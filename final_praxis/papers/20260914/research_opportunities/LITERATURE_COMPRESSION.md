# Compression and laptop inference: research opportunities

Research cutoff: **14 September 2026**. Primary papers, author repositories, model cards, and dataset cards were inspected. This is a literature and artifact review, not a reproduction or novelty certification. No new model inference, paid jobs, or installations were performed.

**Recommendation:** develop the completed CTI and Praxis 008 manuscripts while qualifying a new idea. Your clarified reference is **Alpamys Makazhan's Soup**, an interesting small-GPU **fine-tuning** system. Its strongest new research opening is improving the disk-streaming path while proving training correctness and bounding host/device memory. For a mathematical compression Praxis, **KronQ** is the better verified quantization base. The most direct continuation of your model-decision interests is compression interacting with selectively presented evidence. Merely running an 8B model on a laptop, measuring refusal degradation, or adding a per-prompt compression router is already established work.

## The identified Soup project

The repository is **[MakazhanAlpamys/Soup](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup)**; its author is **Alpamys Makazhan**, its website is **[trysoup.dev](https://trysoup.dev/)**, and its code license is **Apache-2.0**. No separate repository named SoupTraining appeared in the author's complete public repository listing. The inspected source commit is `e2c13ffc866c3c5f91f5b0d7fba2b35a27dcbe32`. This is separate from Wortsman et al.'s [ICML 2022 model-soup averaging](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/wortsman22a.html).

Soup streams a frozen base model from host RAM, or disk, through a small GPU buffer pool while keeping trainable LoRA adapters resident. That reduces **GPU residency**, not the model's parameter count. NF4 can separately reduce the base's storage. Its training headline is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, batch one, sequence length 512, **119.6 training tokens/s and 3.32 GB peak VRAM** on an RTX 3050 Laptop. These are historical v0.72.2 measurements; they are not chat-generation speed or a present-version benchmark. [Original NF4 measurement](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup/blob/main/benchmarks/gate-v0.72.2-nf4.md)

The original machine had **16.9 GB host RAM** as well as the 4-GB GPU. Its approximately **3.60-GB pinned NF4 decoder store** is not total process RAM or the complete checkpoint. Unquantized vocabulary matrices, adapters, activations, allocator overhead, and the OS matter. A separate 8-GB M1 record uses the **MLX training backend**, not evidence that CUDA layer streaming works identically in unified memory. It reports a 5.154-GB peak and substantial memory pressure/paging; it is a short feasibility run, not sustained training validation. [Hardware and evidence index](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md), [M1 record](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup/blob/main/benchmarks/run-m1-8gb-mlx-sft.md)

The v0.73 repair addressed stale NF4-buffer references corrupting **backward gradients at larger per-layer sizes**, despite exact forward results. The reported 8B/14B comparisons were already exact; it would be wrong to say this invalidated the 8B result. Corrected 32B/72B controls were subsequently run. The RTX 3050 headline has not been rerun after that repair, and newer large-vocabulary streaming also changes the memory layout. Thus neither old throughput nor old VRAM should be relabeled as current. [Correctness ledger](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup/blob/main/benchmarks/gate-h100-validation.md)

The accompanying **Exact Layer Streaming: LoRA Fine-Tuning of an 8B Model on a 4 GB Laptop GPU** is a Zenodo preprint. The August 10 v2 is DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21877316`; the concept record currently resolves to the **August 13 record `10.5281/zenodo.21918325`**. No peer-reviewed acceptance was verified. Its downstream base task is **dair-ai/emotion**, with a small fixed test panel; its roughly one-percentage-point resolution does not establish universal quality equivalence. The dataset is downloadable but declares license **“other”**, so do not advertise unrestricted redistribution. Some historical experiment harnesses were not archived, as the repository itself acknowledges. [Preprint record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21918325), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dair-ai/emotion)

The most actionable new observation is dated **September 12**: the shipped disk tier is **2.3–2.8× slower than RAM streaming even with the 7B store cached in host memory**. The maintainer attributes this to synchronous mapped reads on the compute thread. The same record distinguishes warm-cache results from a cold **synthetic 70B-shaped** store; those synthetic timings are not trained 70B-model quality. It also measures RAM streaming as compute/dequantization limited at its main operating point, superseding the earlier blanket transfer-bottleneck explanation. [September 12 probe](https://github.com/MakazhanAlpamys/Soup/blob/main/benchmarks/probe-rtx5070-what-bounds-streaming.md)

**Soup-specific Praxis candidate S1:** introduce a bounded asynchronous disk-prefetch scheduler with explicit buffer-lifetime ownership across forward, recomputation, and backward. **RQ:** can it reduce streamed LoRA training time when the base exceeds available RAM, under fixed host-RAM and VRAM caps, without changing gradients or downstream performance? **H:** it improves useful non-padding training tokens/s over Soup's synchronous disk path and a fixed-depth prefetch baseline, while meeting a preregistered memory bound and resident-reference gradient checks at sizes where the reference fits. Use `dair-ai/emotion` for the base-paper reproduction after clearing its terms, and a separately licensed public task for transfer.

Compare current Soup RAM/disk, resident QLoRA where feasible, fixed prefetch, and a relevant ZeRO/LoHan/MegaTrain offload implementation under matched numerics and constraints. Report warm/cold cache, physical reads, pinned/anonymous/file-backed host memory, allocated/reserved GPU memory, clocks, power, and repeated wall-clock training. Test deliberately stale-buffer controls, resume, accumulation, heterogeneous layers, and disk failures. **Novelty is not asynchronous prefetch itself**—offload systems already use it. The contribution would need a validated scheduling/lifetime method or calibrated memory bound for this low-memory training regime. Mere implementation of an obvious TODO is an engineering project until it shows a generalizable result. [Recent close prior, MegaTrain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05091)

## Colibrì: a different way to use smaller hardware

The matching project is **[JustVugg/colibri](https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri)**, by the developer using the name JustVugg, rather than the unrelated Colibri scientific packages or its many forks. It was created July 1, 2026; the inspected commit is `f028d26b422144ed4a69ad9aeaee2553ce0f9572`, licensed Apache-2.0. This is a public inference engine/research platform; a peer-reviewed Colibrì paper was not verified.

| | Soup | Colibrì |
|---|---|---|
| Main operation in the highlighted result | LoRA fine-tuning | Autoregressive inference |
| What moves | Frozen decoder-layer weights | Router-selected MoE experts |
| Where the rest lives | Host RAM or disk | RAM/VRAM caches and disk |
| What is preserved by placement alone | Base weights; training needs separate gradient validation | Existing quantized weights and router decisions |
| Main resource tradeoff | GPU residency versus transfers/recomputation | Fast-memory residency versus expert fetch latency |

Colibrì's original 744B GLM-5.2 example needs a **roughly 372-GB model container**, despite running on a machine with about 25 GB RAM. Its published cold baseline on that small machine is **0.05–0.1 generated tokens/s**, approximately 10–20 seconds per token. Current examples at several tokens/s include much larger hardware or cache residency; the six-RTX-5090 demonstration is not a laptop result. The small C engine is not the model file. These distinctions make it a capacity achievement, not evidence that the model has been compressed into 25 GB with fast interactive responses. [Benchmark protocol and hardware table](https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri/blob/main/docs/benchmarks.md)

The maintained GLM container and upstream GLM-5.2 model cards both declare MIT; Colibrì's Apache-2.0 code license is separate. Its default placement contract does not silently change the router or precision, but that does **not** remove the original quantization error. Current documentation recommends group-size-64 int4 rather than older per-row containers and records quality differences. Pin the exact model/container, not just “GLM-5.2.” [Recommended container](https://huggingface.co/mastouri/GLM-5.2-colibri-int4-g64-with-int8-mtp), [upstream model](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2)

**Colibrì-specific candidate S2: expert-cache placement that survives workload changes.** The README explicitly requests held-out, cross-session evaluation of learned hot-expert placement versus LRU. **RQ:** can a change-aware placement rule reduce expert-fetch latency after task distribution shifts, under fixed RAM/VRAM/SSD budgets, while preserving the quantized model's routing and outputs? **H:** a prospectively specified placement rule improves held-out p95 token latency and total physical bytes read versus the strongest matched caching/prefetch baseline, without increasing cold-start cost beyond a frozen bound. Combining recent frequency, decayed frequency, and miss cost is an initial comparator, not an established new contribution.

Use the repository's public profiling/quality harnesses and its MMLU/HellaSwag/ARC workloads as a base; reserve disjoint source questions and later sessions before learning expert frequencies. Include coding/chat/multilingual shifts only with documented public sources and terms. Start with a supported smaller MoE for implementation correctness, then validate the mechanism on the named target model if its storage/runtime budget is justified. Compare fixed learned pins, LRU, LFU/decay, a strong cost-aware cache, and an offline perfect-knowledge cache as an **upper bound only**. Measure cold, warm-identical, and warm-new-topic cases separately; include profile-building cost, cache pollution, physical reads, swap, and per-stage timing. Verify identical router selections/expert outputs and deterministic token output under matched kernels; use a preregistered tolerance when exact numerical identity is unavailable.

This is an **attempted contribution, not a verified novel caching algorithm**. Generic expert prefetch, learned frequency, and storage tiers already exist. A Praxis must show a specific adaptation rule improves across held-out sessions/hardware beyond those baselines. A repeated-prompt cache win alone fails the research gate. The project is especially relevant to your “different skills” interest, but MoE experts are numerical subnetworks; a routing visualization does not establish that each is a person-like skill or brain region. [Explicit open hypotheses](https://github.com/JustVugg/colibri#open-hypotheses-experiments-and-how-to-help)

**Named closest priors narrow S2 substantially.** [Lin, He, and Chen, November 8, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05814v1), already compare LFU with LRU and propose combining popularity with inactivity and testing changing workloads. Their speculative analysis predicts experts without executing prefetch transfers. The paper links its upstream implementation; a separate release of its own code and machine-readable traces was not verified. [Suram, August 18, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18261v1), directly tests domain-primed caching and reports no gain over LRU for the tested long domain segments. [MIT code and traces](https://github.com/Shriniwas410/cacheable-by-design) are public, but checkpoints and training corpora are excluded. Its mixed-cache evaluator counts priming separately; it does not measure physical SSD latency.

Consequently, domain switches, frequency/recency scoring, and a favorable cache-hit simulation alone cannot support the novelty claim. Add unmodified LFU, popularity/inactivity, and domain-primed LRU to the mandatory controls. Screen [Fiddler (ICLR 2025)](https://github.com/efeslab/fiddler) and [MoE-Infinity](https://github.com/EfficientMoE/MoE-Infinity) as CPU/GPU orchestration and expert-offload baselines; both publish Apache-2.0 code, but their model/backend compatibility must be qualified, and MoE-Infinity's current engine is not identical to its original paper implementation. Before algorithm development, test whether representative held-out session changes leave meaningful latency headroom after LRU has warmed. Charge priming reads, cache pollution, bandwidth contention, and calibration to the proposed method. If gains vanish outside artificially short segments, or the result only repeats those priors on Colibrì, retain it as a systems replication rather than advance a novel-method Praxis.

## What an 8B model actually requires

For exactly eight billion parameters, the **weight payload alone** is:

| Representation | Decimal GB | GiB |
|---|---:|---:|
| FP32 | 32 | 29.80 |
| FP16/BF16 | 16 | 14.90 |
| 8-bit | 8 | 7.45 |
| 4-bit | 4 | 3.73 |
| 2-bit | 2 | 1.86 |

These are arithmetic lower estimates, not measured process memory. Quantizer scales, zero points, higher-precision tensors, activations, temporary buffers, runtime allocations, and the KV cache add to them. “8B” is rounded; actual tensor counts matter. CPU RAM, discrete GPU VRAM, and Apple unified memory are different budgets. Memory mapping a file does not make its working set free.

For conventional dense attention caches:

`KV bytes = 2 × layers × cached tokens × KV heads × head dimension × bytes/value × batch`.

For example, the **official Qwen3-8B configuration** has 36 layers, eight KV heads, and head dimension 128. At two bytes/value and batch one, that is **144 KiB/token**, or **1.125 GiB at 8,192 tokens** and **4.5 GiB at 32,768 tokens**, before cache metadata and allocation overhead. Weight quantization alone does not reduce this term. [Configuration](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/blob/main/config.json)

Four-bit laptop inference is established in **llama.cpp** and **MLX-LM**, both MIT-licensed frameworks. Offloading moves memory demand between devices and adds transfer costs; unstructured sparsity needs appropriate kernels to produce speed gains; distillation changes the model; model soups merge models; native ternary training is different from post-training quantizing an existing 8B checkpoint. These should not be treated as interchangeable forms of “making the file smaller.” [llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp), [MLX-LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm)

No tokens/second estimate is justified for your laptop until its CPU/GPU, RAM/VRAM, backend, quantization, context length, batch, power mode, and prompt/decode lengths are fixed. Report prefill and decode separately, and measure cold load, warm latency, peak resident memory, and energy where instrumentation is available.

## Three candidate Praxis designs

These are **proposed hypotheses**, not findings. Each needs a final close-prior screen and an artifact qualification gate before scientific compute.

### C1 — Does compression change vulnerability to selective truthful evidence?

**Fit:** strongest continuity with model decision modification and Praxis 008. It studies a deployment interaction, not whether quantization simply reduces aggregate accuracy.

**Public base:** reproduce a small BFCL relevance/function-selection subset used by Wu et al., using the public Apache-2.0 BFCL data and native scorer. For the primary evidence-withholding experiment, reuse the methodology, not the already-exposed outcomes, from Praxis 008 on new source-disjoint MBPP/MBPP+ tasks. MBPP supplies programs/tests and carries CC-BY-4.0; EvalPlus supplies an Apache-2.0 evaluation implementation. HumanEvalPack's 164 Python tasks from the completed study are development-only. [BFCL](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gorilla-llm/Berkeley-Function-Calling-Leaderboard), [MBPP](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/mbpp), [EvalPlus](https://github.com/evalplus/evalplus)

**RQ:** under a fixed local memory/latency envelope, does quantization alter the increase in accepting incorrect revisions caused by supplier-selected passing evidence, and is allocating resources to independent evidence better than allocating them to precision?

**H1:** the selected-versus-random disclosure effect differs between BF16 and compressed versions of the same checkpoint. Use a two-sided, task-paired interaction; do not assume compression always worsens it.

**H2 / process modification:** a calibration-frozen policy allocating a fixed total budget between model precision and independent checker queries reduces erroneous acceptance versus the strongest fixed allocation, with useful-repair acceptance within a preregistered noninferiority margin.

**Design:** cross weight precision with disclosure policy; hold model family, prompt, proposed revision, evidence pool, decoder, and output schema fixed. Independently reserve outcome tests. Report correct→incorrect, incorrect→correct, and unchanged decisions separately. The key estimand is a difference of paired disclosure effects, not accuracy loss alone. Include no-evidence and equal-cardinality/order controls, plus a faithful supplier-policy arm allowing empty evidence when its rule requires it. Price baseline generation, retries, test acquisition, and any escalation. A high-precision reference run is offline experimental instrumentation, not a free runtime feature.

**Baselines:** BF16; W4 and W3; one extra bit; fixed uniform independent queries; edit-aware queries; query-free fixed precision; random budget allocation; the same policy with its decision-sensitive feature removed.

**Novelty risk / stop rule:** Wu already studies tool-decision damage; a public June/July **Safety Invariance** research repository already studies paired executable agent decision flips and failed action-margin selectors. Neither generic paired flips nor mixed precision is new. The candidate contribution is specifically the compression×disclosure interaction and the precision-versus-evidence budget tradeoff. Stop the method claim if the interaction is absent or the learned allocation cannot beat fixed policies. A known-failure veto is a property of a sound checker, not novel empirical defense evidence. [Closest additional artifact](https://github.com/abdulm5/safety-invariance)

### C2 — Cheap, risk-calibrated KV selection after hardware changes

**Fit:** clearest explicit future-work entry point; substantial systems work, moderate mathematical novelty risk.

**Public base:** MetaKV's four datasets—GSM8K, HellaSwag, ARC-Challenge, SQuAD 1.1—and released per-prompt profiles. Start with an independent implementation of its selection rule if reuse terms remain unresolved. Use licensed KIVI as a reproducible runtime component, and retain MetaKV's original configuration pool for a faithful offline baseline. A reduced pool is a declared adaptation, not a full reproduction. [MetaKV §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07966v1), [KIVI code, MIT](https://github.com/jy-yuan/KIVI)

**RQ:** can a small target-device calibration set transfer a KV-selection policy while controlling memory/latency violations, with much less profiling than a full target-device sweep?

**H:** a pooled residual-calibration policy reaches preregistered constrained-success noninferiority to full target profiling at, for example, no more than 20% of its profiling calls, and beats uncalibrated transfer and equal-cost random profiling. The 20% figure is a proposed threshold to freeze after development feasibility, not a measured benefit.

**Mathematical/process modification:** fit hardware-specific scale/offset corrections to source predictions, then use simultaneous calibrated upper bounds for latency and total peak memory across the candidate configurations. Select the most accurate feasible configuration; explicitly abstain when no configuration qualifies. Correctness predictions remain separate from runtime feasibility. Calibrate the selection procedure, not just each predictor independently; ordinary marginal intervals can fail after adaptively selecting the most optimistic arm.

**Baselines and controls:** original MetaKV transfer; full target retraining; fixed conservative margins; device-independent length-only rule; uniform calibration sampling; a strong active profiling baseline with the same labels/cost; the best development-selected static configuration. Split by source problem, then hold out a hardware and workload family. Retain infeasible prompts, abstentions, and out-of-memory failures. Report two outcomes: original KV-budget CSR and deployable **total-memory** CSR. Include model loading, feature extraction, calibration, conversion, and routing costs.

**Novelty risk / stop rule:** adaptive routing itself is MetaKV's contribution. Its authors explicitly request cheaper cross-hardware calibration; conformal methods already appear in compression and routing literature. The contribution must be a demonstrated calibration-efficiency/constraint-control improvement, not relabeling a confidence interval. Distribution shift invalidates blanket exchangeability guarantees: state the calibration population and distinguish stationary guarantees from observed robustness under deliberate drift. Reject the laptop claim if improvements exist only in a replay of server-GPU profiles.

### C3 — Distribution-sensitive mixed precision for consequential decisions

**Fit:** best option if the Praxis must introduce a mathematical quantization modification. It has the strongest verified executable low-bit base, but the highest competition and failure risk.

**Public base:** KronQ's released code, packed Llama-2-7B checkpoint, and WikiText-2 baseline; reproduce its reported configuration before changing the objective. Then add BFCL decision evaluation and one code-decision dataset. A Qwen3-8B extension avoids Llama's custom license, but backend compatibility must be demonstrated rather than assumed. [KronQ reproduction instructions](https://github.com/Intelligent-Computing-Lab-Panda/KronQ), [released checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/donghyunli/Llama-2-7b-KronQ-W4A16), [WikiText](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/wikitext)

**RQ:** at equal packed bytes and a measured latency limit, can a decision-balanced calibration objective allocate precision more robustly across shifts in task mixture than KronQ's original reconstruction/curvature objective?

**H:** a group-robust allocation reduces the worst-group decision error on held-out task families versus original KronQ and a same-budget random allocation, without violating the general-utility noninferiority margin. It must also be compared with simply spending one more bit.

**Proposed math:** replace the single pooled calibration loss used for precision selection with a regularized worst-group objective over decision strata—needed tool calls, irrelevant calls, correct revisions, incorrect revisions—subject to **actual packed storage** and measured kernel costs. One form is `min_b max_g L_g(b) + λ L_general(b)`, constrained by `bytes(b) ≤ M` and `latency(b) ≤ T`. Use KronQ's curvature estimate to propose candidates, then evaluate a small preregistered calibration search. This is an application of robust optimization, not a claim to invent minimax optimization or Hessian-based allocation.

**Baselines:** original KronQ, GPTQ/AWQ, uniform W3/W4, random precision assignment, stronger NVIDIA AutoQuantize or comparable supported mixed-precision search, and a smaller higher-precision model at the same memory. Match calibration token count and search compute. Include loss-reweighting without worst-group optimization and shuffled group labels as ablations. [AutoQuantize, August 24, 2026](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/announcements/autoquantize.html)

**Novelty risk / stop rule:** KronQ already adds gradient covariance; safety-preserving contrastive PTQ, Q-ReAlign, and importance-based protection already exist. A new label on the same objective is insufficient. Reject the method if gains disappear with matched calibration, random allocation, the next bit, or real packed execution. CUDA-only benefit supports a GPU deployment paper, not a CPU-laptop claim. Sherry+KV composition is a separate fallback only after its exact artifacts are available; avoid starting a new ternary pretraining campaign for this Praxis. [Safety-preserving PTQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07842), [Q-ReAlign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08089)

## Reproducibility and investment gates

1. **Artifact gate, before inference:** pin paper version, source commit, checkpoint revision, quantizer settings, tokenizer/template, dataset version, license declaration, calibration IDs, and target hardware. Source availability is distinct from a runnable release and from permission to redistribute.
2. **Measurement gate:** independently test the scorer and packed representation; do not report fake-quantization tensor sizes as deployment RAM savings. Record effective bits including metadata, peak RAM/VRAM, prefill/decode latency, energy where available, and all failed assignments.
3. **Scientific gate:** reserve source-disjoint final tasks before tuning. Cluster intervals by source task; repeated prompts, quantizer seeds, and versions are not new independent tasks. Define one primary effect and a meaningful noninferiority margin. Use executable outcomes where possible; a refusal phrase or a raw-model agreement score alone does not establish safety/correctness.
4. **Investment gate:** advance only after reproducing a public baseline and observing a development signal that survives the next-bit, same-byte, random-allocation, and smaller-model controls. Publish a bounded negative if the method fails; do not select a new primary endpoint after seeing final outcomes.

The July PX055 preflight alone contained no scientific measurements; the [completed PX055 paper in this release](../03_px055/PAPER.md) reports nine geometric cells. Its bounded geometry findings do not establish semantic safety preservation. The new compression proposals require their own novelty and outcome-validation gates. Refusal geometry may remain a secondary diagnostic in C1/C3; causal claims require interventions and decision-level validation.

The companion `SOURCES_COMPRESSION.json` records checked artifacts, pins, licenses, and unresolved release gaps. For the newly clarified projects, **S1 is the stronger training-systems route; S2 is the more direct inference/cache route**. For a mathematical compression contribution, qualify C3 first. For your decision-modification interests, C1 remains the closest fit. The next step is a source-only artifact/closest-prior gate and a frozen hypothesis, not a large unscoped model sweep.
