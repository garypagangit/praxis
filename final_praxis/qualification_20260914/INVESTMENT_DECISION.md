## The next question worth testing

**Research question:** With the same telemetry, alarm budget and inference budget, can a fixed commissioning reference plus constrained cross-channel updates retain detection during persistent attacks better than existing context protection, while recovering after legitimate operating changes?

The trust rule must use information available at the time. It cannot obtain an attack label, known attack onset, future target, or retrospective choice of the best reference. If the design needs a protected sensor or authenticated operating-mode record, that assumption must be explicit and tested when violated. Without distinguishing information, identical malicious and benign observations cannot be distinguished by relabeling them.

**Candidate hypothesis for a new protocol:** Compared with the strongest development-selected deployable baseline, the full method improves **late-episode alarm retention** by at least five percentage points, with a paired episode-level interval excluding zero, while meeting a fixed absolute normal-alarm ceiling and a prespecified benign-recovery noninferiority margin. Define retention as the fraction of observations flagged in a prospectively fixed late portion of each attack episode, then average across episodes with equal weight. Keep episode-onset recall as a supporting noninferiority endpoint, with its margin fixed in advance. The five-point retention margin is a proposed practical threshold, not a literature guarantee. Fix the late portion, alarm ceiling, recovery and onset margins, resource budget, independent units and multiplicity plan before held-out access. This note does not register a confirmatory test.

**Current workload ceiling:** All four original TimesFM 2.5 policies detected 20 of 20 toy attack episodes. Episode-hit recall is therefore saturated on that workload; these observations cannot support a proposed five-point improvement in that endpoint. Prospectively specify persistent, stealthy and benign-recovery development families before testing the new method, and retain the saturated original workload as a reproduction control. This changes the proposed future question, not the completed qualification's metrics or criteria. No favorable result for the new method has been observed or implied.

## Advance and stop rules

1. **Qualification is complete.** Original-model and public-data calibration accounting, causal-window controls, finite outputs, source identity and declared resource gates passed. Preserve the complete raw results, source amendments and disclosed access deviation when defining the next study.
2. **Freeze a narrow development design.** Include deployable alarm-only blending and literal freeze, an equal-budget fixed-plus-rolling reference control, robust/clipped updates, adaptive calibration, and a qualified drift/anomaly comparator. Separate the historical oracle-onset implementation from operational baselines.
3. **Require a discriminating development result before expansion.** The method must beat the strongest simple comparator at matched alarms and compute, retain late-attack detection, and meet the recovery limit. Removing its cross-channel restriction should weaken the relevant result; otherwise that component has no demonstrated contribution. Development success is not held-out confirmation.
4. **Stop or redesign** if the gain exists only against naive rolling context, comes from extra model calls or a higher alarm rate, requires attack labels to admit benign changes, vanishes under slow contamination, or cannot improve on a simple dual-reference/clipping policy. Do not rescue the hypothesis by changing the held-out split or success threshold after outcomes.
5. **Protect the next test's status.** Cite the disclosed HAI timestamp-access deviation when constructing future chronological splits. Sensor/label outcomes must remain unavailable until the new roles and analysis are frozen. Modified versions of one episode do not count as independent incidents.

The [independent novelty challenge](NEXT_DESIGN_NOVELTY_CHALLENGE.md) details the required controls. Close prior work already includes [TimesFM context protection](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06347v1), [reference/recent/retraining windows for contamination versus drift](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02107v1), and [dual drift detection for prolonged anomalies](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03576v3). Adding memories or a weight cap is therefore not enough to claim a new method. An admission-mass cap also does not, by itself, bound nonlinear forecast influence.

The possible contribution is a measured, reproducible improvement in this explicitly limited retention/recovery problem; or a carefully characterized failure boundary. Neither result is promised. No new experiment, model call, data download or cloud job was authorized or performed by preparing this decision.
