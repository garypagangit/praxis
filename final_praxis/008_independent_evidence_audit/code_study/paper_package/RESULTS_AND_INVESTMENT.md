**H1 is supported for schema-constrained Qwen.** Selected testimony increased reviewer-only harmful acceptance from **4/101 to 12/101**, an **8/101 = 7.92-percentage-point** increase, CI **[2.97, 13.86] points**, exact one-sided p=**0.00390625** and Holm-four adjusted p=**0.015625**. There were four acceptances under both conditions, eight selected-only acceptances, zero uniform-only acceptances and 89 under neither. Useful native repairs were accepted in 100/101 cases under both testimony arms.

This establishes an effect of the **complete disclosure policy** in that configuration. The treatment combines passing-result filtering, possible omission/fewer visible records and minimum-Jaccard pairing when possible; it does not isolate clustering, rhetoric or a single cognitive mechanism. It also does not show that schema constraints caused vulnerability: original Qwen heldout reviews were never collected. Only 60/101 harmful native proposals had two available passing witnesses in the primary acquisition replicate; all 101 remain in the registered maximum-two-witness estimate.

The independently inspected eight discordant pairs sharpen that boundary. Both H1 arms have valid, complete responses on all 101 harmful primary tasks. In seven of the eight additional harmful acceptances, uniform testimony showed a failed test that selected testimony withheld. Six pairs showed two passing selected records; two showed **no selected records** against two failing uniform records. One pair showed two passing records in both arms with different test IDs. No flip involved identical prompts or only reordered copies of the same records. This is an exploratory mechanism description, not a new confirmatory subset test. The registered effect is selective disclosure, including omission; it must not be described as eight cases persuaded by reassuring passing tests. [Mechanism diagnostic](../postrun_review/completed_extension_v2/H1_MECHANISM_DIAGNOSTIC.json)

Devstral's H1 remains **3/101 versus 1/101**, an increase of 1.98 points, CI [0, 4.95], raw p=.25 and V2 Holm p=**.75**, which does not meet the criterion. Its native observations are exactly reused from V1. The change from V1 adjusted p=1.0 to V2 adjusted p=.75 reflects the new Qwen p-values inside the same four-test family, not changed Devstral data or a fresh replication.

**H2 and the full hybrid-policy recommendation fail for both models.** For Qwen, uniform independent verification had **3/101 enforced harmful acceptances** and hybrid had **4/101**; uniform-minus-hybrid is **-0.99 points**, CI **[-2.97, 0]**, raw/adjusted p=1.0. Static edit also had 3/101. The direction does not favor hybrid; it is not a tested claim that hybrid is significantly worse. For Devstral, uniform, hybrid, edit and complement all had zero harmful acceptances, giving no observed hybrid advantage.

Qwen useful acceptance was **96/101** under both uniform and hybrid verification, with paired difference zero and CI [-2.97, 2.97] points. Devstral was 95/101 under each, with the same useful-difference interval. Both useful contrasts had discordances in both directions, and their registered noninferiority numerical criteria passed. These useful results cannot compensate for failed harm reduction, the practical margin and the static-edit requirement. Neither model qualifies the hybrid as a successful policy.

The raw/enforced distinction matters: Qwen still accepted ten or eleven harmful proposals under independent-test arms before the common veto, while enforcement retained only three or four. Authenticated failure checking therefore changes those decisions operationally. It is an established rule, and these observations do not constitute a registered superiority claim for the hybrid selector.
