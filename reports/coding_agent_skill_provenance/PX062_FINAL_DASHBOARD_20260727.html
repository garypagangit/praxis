Was this the correct experiment?

Yes — for the registered questions

Gate 1 directly tested whether identity and provenance controls reject the paper's authentic released poisoning artifacts. Gate 2.1 directly tested a deterministic existence check plus a fresh recovery turn on two frozen model families.

The cross-model requirement was scientifically useful: it exposed model dependence that a Qwen-only study would have hidden.

Not a production-agent safety test

No skill was loaded or executed. Recovery omitted the original task and prior assistant turn. The benchmark did not test best-skill recovery, semantic poisoning, runtime compromise, general hallucination, or natural deployment prevalence.

A structured tool-call verifier or context-preserving controller would be a new experiment, not a reinterpretation of this result.

Claim boundary and disposition

What is established: under the frozen prompts, models, registry snapshot, exact-name parser, and decontextualized recovery treatment, Qwen converted nearly all nonexistent-name recommendations to a registered name or abstention, while Mistral did not. Provenance-only admission rejected tampering and nonexistent objects but did not reject authentic signed poisoned content.

What is not established: context-preserving retry performance, correct replacement-skill selection, semantic-poisoning prevention, production compromise prevention, general hallucination rates, or production-agent safety.

Disposition: close this tested cross-model mechanism as a no-go. Do not retune it against the observed outcomes. Any structured or context-preserving replacement is a new preregistered experiment.
