CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background
Standing-Committee Routing in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Models is part of the Praxis Research portfolio, a future-work-to-experiment program for AI/ML and cybersecurity engineering. The portfolio standard is evidence-first: a result is promoted only when it has a defined objective, a reproducible artifact trail, a measured gate, and a written claim boundary.

1.2 Research Motivation
MoE architectures are often described as domain-specialized, but router behavior may centralize computation through repeated expert committees.

1.3 Problem Statement
Claims about specialization need router-level evidence across prompt domains, architectures, and style perturbations.

1.4 Thesis Statement
Sparse MoE routers can exhibit stable high-mass standing committees across prompt domains and style variants, challenging simple domain-specialization assumptions under a frozen audit.

1.5 Research Objectives
1. Capture routed expert committees under a frozen prompt-domain audit.
2. Measure pairwise committee overlap using Jaccard metrics.
3. Replicate across OLMoE base, OLMoE-Instruct, and Qwen1.5-MoE.
4. Position novelty relative to 2026 Standing Committee work.

1.6 Research Questions and Hypotheses
RQ / Hypothesis | Statement
----------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RQ1 / H1 | Do audited MoE models show stable high-overlap committees? Hypothesis: Router capture is at least 0.95 for each audited model.
RQ2 / H2 | Does the effect persist across base/instruct variants? Hypothesis: Primary mean pairwise Jaccard is at least 0.25.
RQ3 / H3 | Does the effect replicate in a Qwen MoE architecture? Hypothesis: Bootstrap CI low is at least 0.20.
RQ4 / H4 | Is the result novel as first discovery or as local confirmation/extension? Hypothesis: Committee-size sensitivity remains above 0.20.

1.7 Scope of Research
Frozen 480-prompt audit: five domains, twelve base prompts per domain, eight style variants per prompt, committee sizes 16/32/64.

1.8 Research Limitations
Not first discovery, not causal expert specialization, not universal MoE behavior, and not behavior-level intervention evidence.

5.2 Contributions to the Body of Knowledge and Practice

2. Cross-model confirmation of standing committees.
3. A clear novelty boundary against current literature.

5.3 Recommendations for Practitioners
1. Audit router traces before assuming domain specialization.
2. Report committee overlap separately from behavioral performance.

2. Test under fine-tuning and domain shift.
