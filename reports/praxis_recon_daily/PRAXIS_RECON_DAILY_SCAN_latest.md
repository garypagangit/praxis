> The growing integration of large language models (LLMs) with multimodal perception, retrieval, persistent memory, and external tools has expanded the attack surface of modern AI systems. Existing studies report vulnerabilities ranging from prompt-based jailbreaks and visual instruction injection to retrieval poisoning, memory manipulation, and unauthorized tool use. However, these findings are reported under heterogeneous taxonomies, threat assumptions, and evaluation protocols, limiting direct comparison of their underlying mechanisms and security consequences. This paper presents a four-dimensional analytical framework for examining security vulnerabilities in LLM-based systems. The framework characterizes each documented attack in terms of its attack surface (AS), exploitation mechanism (EM), security constraint failure (CF), and security impact (SI). We apply this framework to a structured comparative analysis of representative primary studies and publicly available security benchmarks covering conventional LLMs, multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), and agent-based systems.
