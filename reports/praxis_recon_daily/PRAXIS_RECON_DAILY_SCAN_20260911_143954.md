Abstract excerpt:

> Cross-organizational health information exchange preserves clinical meaning but can leave accountability evidence fragmented or excessively revealing. This study develops TrustEvidence, a healthcare audit-evidence boundary with an executable reference profile that classifies event facts as portable, referenced, committed or excluded. An improvement-oriented design-science evaluation used a synthetic disclosure case, a Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources Release 4 projection, official tools, a Merkle receipt, adversarial tests, paired measurements and release reproduction. Four positive units completed the toolchain without fatal or error findings; two intended-negative units were rejected. All 54 registered adversarial rejections were observed. A signed alternative tree-size assertion exposed limits on backend honesty and completeness claims. Against source processing, the path added p50, p95 and p99 increments of 9.023, 9.290 and 9.668 ms. The results support a bounded, reproducible profile, not clinical truth, legal compliance, scalability or hospital readiness.
