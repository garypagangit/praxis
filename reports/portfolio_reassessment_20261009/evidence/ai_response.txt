DECISION
Develop a defensible evaluation and evidence-assessment Praxis. Current results
do not establish a reliable detector of fully autonomous AI versus human APTs.
Stop expanding classifier searches merely to obtain a favorable score. Preserve
negative findings and make the contribution the tested method and its limits.
This is a proposed research scope, not a guarantee of committee acceptance.

PROPOSED TITLE
Assessing the Reliability of Behavioral AI-Operator Attribution for SOC Triage
Using Existing Public Cybersecurity Benchmarks

PROBLEM
Before a SOC relies on an AI-operator alert, it needs evidence that the detector
recognizes operator behavior across unfamiliar tasks and operators rather than
recording conventions, benchmark artifacts, or familiar command vocabularies.
False AI accusations and missed AI activity both matter. These experiments test
that reliability problem using existing records. They do not test analyst gains.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS AND HYPOTHESES
RQ1: How much comparable command and response evidence can be recovered from
existing human and autonomous-agent records, and what exclusions affect it?
This is a descriptive measurement question, not a hypothesis of high accuracy.

RQ2: Do behavior and response features improve attribution beyond verb-frequency
baselines when tasks and human experts are held out?
H1: Adding response features improves AI recall without increasing equal-expert
human false-positive rate at thresholds fixed using calibration humans.
PX125 does not support H1 in its pooled pilot. No significance claim is made.

RQ3: Does attribution retain useful performance on unfamiliar benchmarks or AI
families, and what tradeoff follows when a system declines uncertain decisions?
H2: A fixed procedure attains at least 80% AI recall with at most 5% equal-expert
human false-positive rate on held-out evaluations.
The tested PX123/PX124/PX125 candidates do not meet both point targets.
These are exploratory operational targets, not a validated population guarantee.

RQ4: What evidence should accompany an attribution suggestion before SOC use?
Deliverable: an evidence-report specification covering provenance, extraction
quality, coverage, model applicability, false-positive evidence and uncertainty.
Actual analyst improvement remains untested. Do not formulate it as a proven H3.

LATEST EXPERIMENT: PX125
Existing Lyptus records: InterCode-CTF, CyBench and CVE-Bench; pinned source
178c0da1800f9c07311e1b8d6c61dd8104e7b4ae. No new attack collection.
333 sessions inspected. 38 human and 56 AI sessions pass response-coverage rules.
Matching tasks leaves 22 human and 26 AI sessions across 19 tasks, four human
experts. Only two human sessions and five Google sessions contain observed
error-followed-by-action pairs. Missing or compound-command responses are not
silently converted to no-error observations.

Pooled results (30 AI evaluations from 26 distinct AI sessions; 22 human sessions):
Method                AI detected   Human false AI flags   Equal-expert human FPR
Verb frequency         5/30           6/22                   33.3%
Behavior              22/30           5/22                   22.9%
Behavior + response   20/30           6/22                   35.4%
Combined              21/30           7/22                   37.5%
All four experts receive equal weight for the final FPR column. AI sessions can
appear in more than one expert test fold; evaluations are not independent trials.
Response features fail to improve both recall and false flags over behavior.
All 12 held-family method summaries also miss the joint point target. One
Anthropic/expert fold is infeasible and skipped under the frozen rules.
These small, selected, previously exposed data cannot validate general absence
of a useful signal. They do reject claiming success from the present models.

WHAT THE DATA SUPPORT
- A reproducible comparison with explicit task/operator separation and coverage.
- Evidence that initially promising attribution scores do not by themselves
  establish robust transfer or low human false-positive rates.
- A measured limitation of response-based attribution in these public records.

WHAT THE DATA DO NOT SUPPORT
- Identifying fully AI-driven real-world APT campaigns from these bounded tasks.
- A verified distinction between autonomous, assisted and wholly human campaigns.
- A production-safe 5% false-positive guarantee or measured SOC time savings.
- Claiming error recovery or abstention itself as novel without prior-art review.

CONCRETE SOC RESEARCH ARTIFACT
An attribution evidence report should expose: input provenance and authorized
scope; number of observable commands/pairs; missing/compound exclusions; whether
the evaluation covers this source/family; feature evidence; human false-positive
and AI-recall results with denominators; and an explicit insufficient-evidence
status. Current candidates warrant insufficient evidence for operational
operator attribution. Do not turn a logistic score into a certified probability
of AI authorship, or equate a human-origin label with APT membership.
This is an artifact specification; no SOC deployment or analyst study is claimed.
