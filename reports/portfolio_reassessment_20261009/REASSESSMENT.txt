PRAXIS PORTFOLIO REASSESSMENT — OCTOBER 9, 2026

Decision

The portfolio contains viable applied research directions. Several broad negative labels overstated what had failed. A small credible benefit, a useful characterization, or a well-controlled application of an established method can support a Praxis. Neither a new architecture nor a universal performance win is necessary. This is an assessment of scientific plausibility, not a guarantee of committee acceptance or a declaration of firstness.

The latest AI data recommendation was premature before verifying a fair comparison dataset. That qualification gap does not refute SOC assistance. However, existing matched transfer experiments also show substantial attribution errors: this is more than an access problem. We should pursue an evidence-aware analyst aid only with the analyst benefit treated as an unanswered research question.

Scope and traceability

INVENTORY.csv/INVENTORY.json cover 118 identified PX records (001–072 and 080–125), ten separate FINAL records, and six suite-level records: 134 entries, not 134 independent hypotheses or datasets. PX-034 duplicates PX-003; multiple later studies reuse earlier data. PX-073–079 were not found in inspected registries and are not fabricated as failed studies. PX-070, PX-071 and the Plan B suite have no reconciled final efficacy determination in this review; they remain explicitly unresolved rather than silently rejected.

This is a record-level portfolio reassessment with focused reconciliation of decision sources, later overrides, comparators and selected arithmetic. It is not a rerun of every model, an independent laboratory replication, or a systematic literature review. EVIDENCE_INDEX.json binds 21 copied decision documents to original paths, checkout commits, tracked/dirty status and SHA-256. Untracked later evidence is preserved as a snapshot rather than falsely attributed to the checkout's commit. These copies may contain relative links intended for their original directories; use the index for original provenance. Earlier registries and preregistered decisions remain unchanged.

The October 9 data qualification is in ../research_readiness_20261009. It documents 93 verified local archives, cohort definitions, public-source access and the bounded AWS inventory. No new models or cloud workloads were started for this reassessment. The existing data-loader instance was running; that does not imply all AWS spending is zero.

What changes in our judgment

1. FINAL-002: an absolute 90% clean-completion target rejected a containment method whose clean completion equaled its baseline at 85%. Reopen the bounded security improvement. Zero observed difference is not population noninferiority; the original gate still failed.
2. PX-061: a 1.42 percentage-point gain missed a 2-point threshold. Reopen as a modest development signal, while recognizing very low absolute accuracy and uncertain replication.
3. PX-087 and APT fusion: a modeled 7.09% cost reduction or 0.92-point macro-F1 gain can be useful. Neither needs a large effect to deserve qualification.
4. Applied novelty: the presence of existing speculative decoding, provenance verification, RAG, fingerprinting or constrained classification does not automatically close an application. The contribution must be the actual target problem, evaluation, useful artifact or measured benefit. Renaming a dataset alone does not establish one.
5. Certificate checker: the original practical threshold was unattainable from the observed baseline. That reason for rejection was defective. Independently, the checker tied the score-only baseline, so added benefit still has not been shown.
6. Keep genuine negative comparisons: corrected PX-057 loses to fixed-two answers on both accuracy and token savings; PX-103 XAI ties confidence; response-conditioned AI features do not improve the comparator. A smaller desired improvement cannot reverse these facts.
7. Data blocks and invalid instruments are separate: zero matched tasks, missing timing, no install actions, no correct candidate, or zero baseline attack harm cannot answer the intended efficacy question.

Plausible shortlist — existing evidence, possible claim, remaining work

A. SOC evidence-aware AI-assistance triage (PX-117–125, 100–102; preferred direction)
Current evidence: behavioral fingerprints exist, but later matched and transfer checks expose false attribution and fragile calibration. No analyst benefit has been measured. Data provenance, replay, applicability and evidence reporting are useful existing assets.
Claim to test: a structured evidence report helps analysts make fewer unsupported AI-origin judgments, choose better next evidence, or finish comparable investigations faster, without materially reducing correct threat recognition.
Applied contribution: integrate established indicators into a calibrated, uncertainty-aware SOC workflow and evaluate the human decision, including when the correct answer is insufficient evidence. No new transformer is required.
Remaining: common-recording human, ordinary automation, hybrid and AI cases; independent actor/task/environment partitions; fair alert+logs and OHAI-checklist baselines; source/length controls; then a blinded counterbalanced analyst study. A prospective modest time/error benefit is sufficient if credible. Detailed plan: AI_SOC_PLAN.txt.
Priority: first for user fit; prospective, not the most completed positive result.

B. Cascade containment with preserved task utility (FINAL-002)
Current evidence: in one-model toy security workflows with controlled field corruption, compromise events are 10/60 ungated versus 0/60 gated; clean tasks 51/60 in both. Paired compromise-difference interval in original report is 8.33–26.67 percentage points. Downstream propagation is zero for the stronger containment arms.
Claim available: a bounded controlled containment result without observed additional clean-task loss in this evaluation. Original absolute utility gate remains failed.
Remaining: determine whether simple action validation provides the same protection; independent tasks, realistic injected failures and another model/workflow; uncertainty on clean-utility differences. Final action rejection is partly enforced by construction, so emphasize end-to-end task and propagation evidence.
Priority: strongest obvious threshold-based reopening.

C. Cheaper evidence acquisition while preserving warnings (PX-087)
Current evidence: normalized modeled cost 1.0000 to 0.9291 (7.09% lower), same reported exfiltration/movement warning totals and false alarms; macro-F1 0.713604 to 0.713508.
Claim to test: an established early-exit/acquisition policy reduces measured latency, calls or resource cost at fixed useful warnings and review burden.
Remaining: real resource measurements, stronger cheap/anytime baselines, incident-level warning equivalence and independent capture. Modeled cost is not measured runtime or analyst savings.
Priority: inexpensive applied engineering study with an existing signal.

D. APT training-data allocation and stage fusion (SUITE-APT-BENIGN/FUSION)
Current evidence: adding normal fitting data reduces normal false alarms 10.04% to 0.40% and raises macro-F1 0.4421 to 0.6543, but lateral any-attack detection falls 94.24% to 83.06%. Separately, general+specialist averaging raises macro-F1 0.67486795 to 0.68407161 (+0.920366 percentage points) across three support fits. These are different experiments, not one joint benefit.
Claim available: controlled tradeoffs of label allocation, ordinary training weights and stage-specific score combination in an APT flow setting. Small fusion gains count. Do not claim all-stage protection or early detection.
Remaining: matched label/tuning/compute budgets, independent attack executions, stage and episode outcomes, fixed operating-point comparisons. Existing source data are exposed; repeated seeds are not independent incidents. Lateral policy selected only six of ten primary seeds and still missed its original joint objective. The external four-flow execution cannot establish generalization.
Priority: credible application study with an existing manuscript and honest tradeoffs.

E. Selective executed-test evidence in AI code review (FINAL-008)
Current evidence: on 101 held-out native code pairs, Qwen harmful-patch acceptance rises from 4/101 under uniform evidence to 12/101 under selected evidence: +7.92 points, reported 95% interval 2.97–13.86, multiplicity-adjusted p=.015625. Devstral does not confirm the effect. The tested defense does not outperform its control.
Claim available: a bounded model-dependent vulnerability to evidence selection, including omission of failing tests. It does not isolate a unique geometric-selection mechanism or prove a defense.
Remaining: independent model/task replication, complete-evidence and simple selection/omission controls, and external validation. A negative defense result can accompany a valid vulnerability/evaluation contribution.
Priority: among the most developed bounded empirical results; less aligned with AI-APT.

F. AI-family fingerprints under reduced logging (operator-signal/defense suites)
Current evidence: AI-only held-environment lexical mean accuracy 89.82%, worst environment 81.51%; retrained 32-character truncation mean 86.31%, worst 80.49%. Recovery-order claims weaken under frequency/shuffle controls. Unknown-family handling remains unreliable.
Claim to test: which terminal log fields are worth retaining for conditional AI-family analysis and how much performance is traded for reduced collection/storage.
Remaining: matched telemetry, contemporary held-out model versions/scaffolds, real storage/latency/privacy accounting and unknown-family abstention. The Lyptus family study is separately trained, not frozen Honey-to-Lyptus transfer.
Priority: narrower companion to A. Knowing which AI family acted is conditional on AI involvement; it is not proof an attack was AI-driven.

G. Warning-preservation evaluation for detector updates (PX-086, 094, 100–106)
Current evidence: exact-class negative-flip counting can miss loss of warnings that previously had the wrong attack-stage label; one seed shows 893 of 901 lost exfiltration warnings already had wrong-stage predictions. Other seeds have different counts. Union adds flow alerts but no additional proxy episodes in the tested sources.
Claim available: a reproducible evaluation method that exposes security-relevant warning loss hidden by common aggregate/class metrics.
Remaining: independent incident labels, compare standard binary recall/alert-continuity measures, and show whether the report changes analyst/model-release decisions. Do not claim a new metric family if established binary accounting suffices; the applied audit may still contribute.
Priority: plausible measurement Praxis, not improved detection.

H. CTI evidence compatibility and failure boundaries (PX-003/034/068, later CTI branches)
Current evidence: large original evidence-assisted gains, but on 1247 external cases evidence-only loses 2.33 points for Llama and 1.84 for Qwen. The revised exposed checker has mixed/no gain.
Claim available: characterize when retrieved relationship evidence corrects versus damages CTI answers; evaluate a source-compatibility workflow if one improves a held-out task.
Remaining: fresh sources and questions, leakage/source-conflict controls, retrieval-only/simple-rule baselines, and a frozen policy. Reusing external failures to tune a checker requires a new test set. Generic RAG is not new; a substantiated CTI application can be.
Priority: viable bounded empirical study; do not advertise the current checker as a solution.

I. Privacy-paid adaptive noise allocation (PX-061)
Current evidence: +1.42 points versus equal noise at matched privacy, four of five partitions positive; accuracy only 14.31% versus nonprivate 82.40%. Static unequal noise does not help.
Claim to test: modest utility improvement from low-dimensional private adaptation at a fixed privacy budget.
Remaining: uncertainty over independent partitions, competent tuned DP baselines, more useful operating range, fresh dataset/held-out federation, unchanged privacy accounting. Do not spend on large confirmation before inexpensive baseline qualification.
Priority: reopen, but low absolute utility makes it lower priority than B–D.

J. Quantization and refusal-geometry characterization (PX-055)
Current evidence: later R3 completes nine model/precision cells with integrity pass; registered bounded precision-invariance result and minimum directional ratio 0.975. Behavior association is descriptive across only three models. Restoration proxy is not positive.
Claim available: characterize which refusal-direction measurements remain stable across tested precisions, and where geometric stability fails to imply behavioral safety.
Remaining: independent model/quantizer versions, semantic behavior scoring and current prior-art positioning. No safety-restoration or attack-prevention claim.
Priority: completed bounded characterization; weaker SOC alignment.

Other plausible but lower-priority applications

These are not discarded as impossible. INVENTORY.csv supplies individual evidence boundaries.
- PX-002: bounded profile retrieval; fresh profiles and comparison with ordinary overlap lookup; no actor identity claim.
- PX-004/011/056: source/registry verification; independent natural examples, semantic label audit and task-completion comparison with ordinary lookups. Strong structured results do not imply arbitrary factual truth.
- PX-005/054/058/060: routing, geometry, explanation stability and prediction-versus-interpretation measurement; causal/independent validation needed before operational claims. PX-060 clean prediction gain does not imply direction identification or deletion robustness.
- PX-001: original adaptation gain with an independent negative is a credible boundary study; renewed improvement needs a fresh safe-fallback comparison, not hiding the failed transfer.
- PX-035: 3.45% token saving at equal 9.06% strict accuracy; establish a useful-quality task and stronger short-answer control first.
- PX-059 and FINAL-009: established speculative decoding/prefetch in a new constrained workload can be applied research, but must first show real workload headroom; the measured warm-cache prefetch is slower.
- PX-062: model-specific skill-existence recovery may be useful; context-preserving repair, fresh tasks and strong registry controls required. Provenance cannot certify authentic content as benign.
- PX-088/102 and FINAL-001: review/verification assistance could help people, but current simulations or malformed outputs do not measure that benefit.
- PX-110: controlled DNS study supports baseline characterization; external attacks, prevalence and ordinary detection controls needed.
- PX-112/114: explanation batching/deferment or receipt checking could save resources; quantify actual costs and compare equivalent outputs before claiming a gain.
- PX-051/052: policy/provenance prototypes depend on valid natural labels and traces; current constructed data limit conclusions.
- PX-016/081/096: measured tradeoffs might suit a specific use case; fresh fixed-budget comparison and complete harm accounting needed.

Untested proposals in INVENTORY (robotics, video, world models, graphs, causal learning and others) remain possible ideas. Missing data or large scope does not establish invalidity, but they currently have less evidence and lower fit than the shortlist. We should not repeatedly pivot to a new architecture before qualifying the problem, data and comparator.

Novelty and current source checks

TRACE explicitly studies model-family attribution among AI attack agents, including unseen scaffolds; it does not establish our human-versus-script-versus-hybrid operating point: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01186
OHAI provides a human-to-autonomous attribution framework and heuristics; the inspected report is not a labeled session benchmark or measured validation of our proposed aid: https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/observational-humanai-ohai-a-defender-attribution-framework-for-distinguishing-human-vs-ai-threats/
Few-shot network-attack work already discusses additional benign support; our contribution can be its controlled consequences for APT stage visibility, not originating the suggestion: https://www.techscience.com/CMES/v147n1/67129/html
These primary pages were rechecked October 9. The local studies also retain their own literature reviews. Prior work on counterfactual-risk RAG and utility-enhanced private federated learning was checked during this review (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01302 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21154). None of these checks proves an unoccupied literature gap. Before a new confirmatory study, map its precise applied claim against the closest methods and datasets, rather than requiring a wholly original idea.

Recommendation

Keep A as the preferred Praxis direction, with G/F as existing measurement assets and a tightly scoped human-benefit endpoint. Keep B as the strongest recovered containment result, C/D as practical improvement candidates, and E as a mature bounded empirical alternative. Finish the evidence plan before another training campaign. Do not count a drafted aid, a new feature, or successful data extraction as demonstrated SOC benefit.
