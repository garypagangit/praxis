# Positive Praxis evidence: five directions, with their limits

September 18, 2026. Local portfolio audit, including the completed CTI external test. This is a research-priority judgment, not an academic novelty certification. No historical result or frozen endpoint was changed. Prior studies were inspected; selected saved counts were independently recomputed by `recount_portfolio.py`.

## Recommendation

**The portfolio does not contain five independently validated, novel, successful solutions.** It does contain several useful positive findings, one small working software-reference guard, and a promising registry pilot. Rank the directions by the next question worth answering, while keeping those distinctions visible.

| Priority | Direction and simple problem | Positive measured evidence | What remains unresolved | Recommended next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Priority 1 is the strongest completed empirical risk result. Priority 2 has the largest useful-evidence effect and the clearest active engineering problem, but its new checker failed. Priority 3 is easiest to explain as a working bounded guard. Priority 4 is a next-stage pilot. Priority 5 remains a reserve until its originality and measurement gaps are resolved. This ordering reflects the available evidence and practical next steps, not a formal weighted score.

## Subsequent checker development result

The separately frozen answer-change NLI revision has now completed on the already examined SecEval questions. Llama improved by +0.24 percentage points and Qwen changed by -0.08 points; the paired mean was +0.08 points, 95% CI [-0.24, +0.40]. The checker accepted eight changes per model, corrected eight answers in total, introduced six errors, and rejected 89 of the 97 available useful corrections. It did not establish a reliable improvement across both models. This exposed-data development result leaves the original external failure unchanged and reinforces the need to review evidence applicability and retrieval before another intervention. See the [revision report](../cti_checker_revision_20260918/REPORT.md) and [full results](../cti_checker_revision_20260918/RESULTS.json). The machine-readable portfolio recount preserves the historical source-study counts; the new revision has its own independent audit.

## New post hoc diagnostic: source-locked citation labels

The original source-locked verifier correctly accepted 202/250 constructed supported rows and rejected 250/250 shifted-source rows: 452/500 = 90.4%. A read-only recount reproduces those saved results.

Using the same saved fields, a comparator that accepts exactly when source identity and year match and title similarity is at least .90 obtains **500/500** correct on the constructed labels. Source identity alone obtains **488/500**. The metadata comparator omits the model-generated content check; its success is consistent with negatives built by pairing a claim with a different source. This benchmark therefore does not show incremental semantic-checker benefit over straightforward metadata matching.

This comparator was evaluated **after** the outcomes were known. It is a diagnostic, not a new preregistered result, independent validation, or a reason to rewrite the historical PASS. The benchmark's gold labels describe source-pair construction, not independently judged semantic correctness. The archived score and this limitation must both remain visible. Source hashes and exact recounts are in [EVIDENCE_AUDIT.json](EVIDENCE_AUDIT.json); the audit does not make any model or network calls.

## Decision boundary

The top five are five directions with different levels of evidence. None has human academic approval recorded here. A newer model, additional engineering, or a positive submetric does not establish novelty. The separately prepared literature review controls current overlap claims; no new algorithm is declared novel by this local evidence audit.
