\chapter{Retrieval-Conditioned CTI Compliance}

\section*{Chapter Abstract}

Large language models are increasingly used for cyber threat intelligence, but broad cyber role prompts do not reliably supply answer-bearing ATT\&CK facts.
This chapter evaluates a narrow retrieval-conditioned prompting protocol for strict CTI multiple-choice compliance.
On a locked 106-row, no-label, evidence-addressable CTI-MCQ slice, relationship evidence from MITRE ATT\&CK improves Llama-3.1-8B strict accuracy from 0.642 to 0.915, with no invalid-rate regression and 33 evidence-only paired wins versus 4 vanilla-only wins.
The effect replicates on Llama-3.2-3B, improving from 0.547 to 0.887.
A slice audit gives a soft pass because the complement slice has lower vanilla accuracy, and the ablation bounds the mechanism because technique-only evidence also improves over vanilla.
The final claim is therefore retrieval-conditioned CTI compliance under a locked protocol, not SEC-LoRD extraction and not pure relationship causality.

\section*{Role in the Dissertation}

This chapter is a second narrow positive result in the portfolio after Praxis 06.
Its role is to show that a failed broad prompt-seeding direction can be reframed into a defensible retrieval-conditioned CTI task-compliance result.
The contribution is intentionally smaller than open-ended CTI analysis: the model must answer a locked multiple-choice task with a strict parser, and the paper keeps the evidence-addressable slice boundary visible.
