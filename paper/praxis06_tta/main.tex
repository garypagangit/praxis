\title{Selective Test-Time Adaptation for Streaming APT Stage Detection Under Source-File Shift}
\author{Praxis 06 Draft}

\begin{abstract}
Advanced persistent threat (APT) stage detectors degrade when deployment streams differ from the capture sources, days, and host or network conditions seen during training.
This paper evaluates a deliberately narrow form of no-label test-time adaptation (TTA) for streaming APT stage detection.
The method adapts batch-normalization statistics at inference time but applies a validation-selected selective gate that protects confident Data Exfiltration predictions and overrides only a small fraction of uncertain rows.
On the locked Unraveled held-out source-file split, selective TTA improves Macro-F1 from 0.7685 to 0.8658 and Reconnaissance F1 from 0.0250 to 0.5050, with an override rate of 4.7 percent.
A matched-rate confidence-rejection baseline does not explain the result: it leaves Reconnaissance F1 at 0.0000.
The PR-AUC change is only +0.0006, so the contribution is framed as a safety-constrained operating-point improvement, not as evidence that the underlying representation learns a broadly better Reconnaissance ranking at test time.
The result is defense-ready but intentionally bounded: DAPT2020 supports detector-recipe transfer only, while its TTA feasibility gate is negative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

APT detection systems are commonly evaluated under static train/test assumptions, yet practical deployments face source-file, day, host, and traffic-shape shift.
Stage detection makes this harder than ordinary binary intrusion detection.
An analyst-facing detector must distinguish Benign, Reconnaissance, Foothold, Lateral Movement, and Data Exfiltration behavior, but the rare stages are also the stages most likely to collapse when the deployment stream changes.

The motivation for this study comes from a prior negative result.
Praxis 04 tested stage-conditional routing and found that predicted-stage routing did not survive held-out source-file shift.
The Treatment-Stage router underperformed the baseline, and rare-stage behavior remained brittle.
That failure suggests that the deployment bottleneck is not merely a missing router or a better threshold; the model's operating point must be robust when the target stream shifts.

This paper studies selective no-label TTA for streaming APT stage detection.
The frozen detector is a support-floor MLP trained before deployment.
At test time, the method updates only batch-normalization behavior over the unlabeled stream.
A conservative gate then decides whether to keep the frozen prediction or accept the adapted prediction.
The gate is asymmetric by design: it permits targeted Reconnaissance rescue, but it protects high-consequence Data Exfiltration behavior.

The contribution is intentionally modest.
We do not claim that TTA generally solves APT detection.
We show that, under one locked source-file split and one audited feature pipeline, a validation-selected selective gate can recover a rare shifted stage without reducing mean Data Exfiltration F1.
This is the right kind of result for a doctoral defense: narrow, repeatable, and explicit about its boundaries.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{APT stage detection and deployment shift.}
APT stage detectors aim to provide analysts with more actionable structure than binary intrusion labels.
Recent stage-oriented work such as TSE-APT emphasizes that multi-stage classification can support richer detection workflows \cite{cheng2025tseapt}.
However, stage structure also increases brittleness because rare classes and source-specific telemetry patterns can dominate the failure mode.
The local Praxis 04 negative result is therefore important context: routing on predicted stage failed under held-out source-file shift, motivating adaptation rather than another stage router.

\paragraph{Test-time adaptation.}
TTA methods adapt a model at deployment time using unlabeled target data.
Test-time training uses self-supervised objectives to improve generalization under shift \cite{sun2020testtime}, while Tent adapts by entropy minimization during inference \cite{wang2021tent}.
This paper uses the same broad premise--unlabeled target streams contain useful shift information--but makes a different security-ML design choice.
Adaptation is treated as unsafe unless constrained by a gate that protects high-consequence classes.

\paragraph{Rare-class handling.}
The frozen detector lineage already includes conventional rare-class machinery, including oversampling and weighted loss.
This connects the work to class-imbalance methods such as SMOTE \cite{chawla2002smote} and focal loss \cite{lin2017focal}.
The key point is that static rare-class handling did not solve the deployment operating point for Reconnaissance: the best frozen support-floor detector still collapses to Recon F1 0.0250 on the locked test split.
Selective TTA is therefore positioned as a deployment-time operating-point intervention, not as a replacement for training-time imbalance handling.

\paragraph{Provenance context.}
Provenance-based intrusion systems such as MAGIC and Kairos show the broader direction for host and graph evidence \cite{jia2024magic,cheng2024kairos}.
Those systems motivate future provenance experiments in this portfolio, but they are not evidence for the TTA claim here.
The current provenance track remains label-blocked, so this paper stays with the audited Unraveled flow-feature pipeline.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Frozen Detector}

The frozen detector is a support-floor MLP from the trusted Unraveled feature lineage.
The detector recipe uses targeted ADASYN plus weighted cross-entropy and was selected before the final TTA proof.
The frozen model supplies baseline class probabilities, confidence values, and Data Exfiltration guard decisions.

\subsection{No-Label BatchNorm Adaptation}

The selected adaptation method is \texttt{bn\_adapt}.
During inference, the method updates batch-normalization behavior over the unlabeled evaluation stream.
No test labels are used, and classifier weights are not retrained on labeled target examples.

The locked replay uses a single pass over the evaluation stream with batch size 4096.
The stream is consumed in dataframe order through a non-shuffled data loader.
The model is put in evaluation mode, then only \texttt{BatchNorm1d} modules are toggled to train mode so their running statistics update; dropout remains disabled.
The MLP uses PyTorch \texttt{BatchNorm1d} defaults, including the default momentum, and the frozen checkpoint is reloaded before each split and method evaluation.

\subsection{Selective Gate}

The gate is named \texttt{recon\_guarded}.
It compares frozen and adapted predictions under validation-selected thresholds and applies three constraints:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item preserve confident frozen Data Exfiltration predictions;
  \item allow overrides on uncertain frozen predictions;
  \item allow Reconnaissance rescue only when adapted confidence is sufficient.
\end{enumerate}

The locked policy uses \texttt{bn\_adapt}, a Data Exfiltration delta guard of 0.05, and seeds 42, 43, and 44.
Per-seed thresholds were selected on validation artifacts before final replay.
The final test replay did not run a new broad threshold search.

\subsection{Graphical Model Representation}

Figure~\ref{fig:method} is the operational graphical model representation.
The frozen model emits class probabilities for each incoming row.
The adaptation branch updates only batch-normalization statistics using the unlabeled stream.
The selective gate combines frozen confidence, adapted confidence, and class-specific safety rules to decide whether the final label remains frozen or is overridden.
The gate is not learned on the test set.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset and Split}

The evaluation uses Unraveled network-flow telemetry processed through the trusted support-floor feature pipeline.
The locked split holds out source files to approximate deployment shift.
Train, validation, and test source files are disjoint.
Temporal delta features are reset within each split so cross-split temporal state cannot leak into validation or test.

\subsection{Metrics and Baselines}

Primary metrics are Macro-F1, Reconnaissance F1, Data Exfiltration F1, PR-AUC, and override rate.
Macro-F1 and Reconnaissance F1 capture rare-stage recovery.
Data Exfiltration F1 captures the safety constraint.
PR-AUC is retained to show whether ranking quality changes materially.

The main comparison is against the frozen MLP.
A matched confidence-reject baseline rejects the same fraction of rows as the selective TTA gate but does not use adapted probabilities.
This controls the simplest alternative explanation: that the gain comes from withholding difficult low-confidence rows.

\section{External Validity}

DAPT2020 supports only an appendix-level detector-recipe check, not TTA generality.
The same MLP recipe reaches Macro-F1 $0.6353 \pm 0.0043$ and Reconnaissance F1 $0.8932 \pm 0.0089$ on DAPT2020.
However, the DAPT TTA feasibility gate is negative: the validation-selected TENT variant reduces test Macro-F1 by 0.2874 and Reconnaissance F1 by 0.6589 relative to frozen.
The DAPT test split also has only two Data Exfiltration examples, so it cannot support a DE safety claim.

A lightweight feature-shift diagnostic suggests one plausible mechanism boundary.
Unraveled has moderate train-to-test feature-scale shift: median absolute log standard-deviation ratio is 0.1192, with 11 of 67 features above a twofold standard-deviation ratio.
DAPT2020 has similar median shift but extreme tail instability: p90 absolute log standard-deviation ratio is 26.9379, with 16 of 82 features above the twofold mark.
This supports the cautious hypothesis that BN adaptation helps when target-stream feature scales are compatible with the source-trained normalization layers and can fail when a small set of features has extreme scale mismatch or unstable batch composition.

\section{Threats to Validity}

The positive claim is one dataset family, one feature pipeline, and one locked source-file split.
Held-out source-file shift is a deployment proxy, not live SOC deployment.
The validation split is Recon-heavy relative to the test split, and the sensitivity check shows that threshold choice is not perfectly stable.
BN adaptation assumes useful structure in the unlabeled stream and could be manipulated by an adversary.
Adversarial stream poisoning was not tested.
DAPT2020 is negative for TTA, so this paper cannot claim cross-dataset TTA generality.

\section{Claims Not Made}

This paper does not claim that TTA works across all APT datasets.
It does not claim that PR-AUC or representation quality materially improves.
It does not claim that the method is robust to adversarial stream poisoning.
It does not use DAPT2020 as positive TTA evidence.
It does not claim the provenance graph experiments are positive.
These limits are part of the contribution: the positive claim is narrow, reproducible, and bounded by the observed failures.

\section{Conclusion}

Selective no-label TTA can recover a rare APT stage under a locked held-out source-file split when adaptation is constrained by a validation-selected safety gate.
The strongest version of the claim is narrow and practical: the method improves the final decision policy for Reconnaissance without relying on labels at test time and without being reducible to confidence rejection.
The result should be submitted as a constrained security-ML adaptation finding, with DAPT2020 retained as an honest boundary on generality.
