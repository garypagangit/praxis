\begin{abstract}
Advanced persistent threat detectors face source-file and temporal shift, especially for rare attack stages such as Reconnaissance.
This paper evaluates a narrow, safety-constrained form of no-label test-time adaptation for streaming APT stage detection.
The method adapts batch-normalization statistics at test time but applies a validation-selected selective gate that preserves confident Data Exfiltration predictions and only overrides a small fraction of uncertain rows.
On the locked Unraveled held-out source-file split, selective TTA improves Macro-F1 from 0.7685 to 0.8658 and Reconnaissance F1 from 0.0250 to 0.5050, with an override rate of 4.7 percent.
A matched-rate confidence rejection baseline does not explain the result: it leaves Reconnaissance F1 at 0.0000.
The claim is intentionally narrow: this is an operating-point and decision-policy improvement under a locked split, not a general proof that TTA improves all APT datasets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

APT detection systems must operate under source-file, host, and temporal shift.
Rare stages are the most fragile part of this setting: a detector may have useful probability rankings overall while still failing the operational decision point for a stage such as Reconnaissance.
The contribution here is a selective no-label adaptation protocol that constrains test-time adaptation with a safety gate.

The paper makes four claims:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Selective TTA improves locked held-out source-file Macro-F1.
  \item Selective TTA recovers Reconnaissance under this split.
  \item The gate preserves Data Exfiltration on average under the declared guard.
  \item The effect is not equivalent to rejecting uncertain frozen predictions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

The closest adaptation baseline is Tent, which performs fully test-time adaptation by entropy minimization \cite{wang2021tent}.
Test-time training and related adaptation methods motivate the general idea that unlabeled test streams can expose distribution-shift structure \cite{sun2020testtime}.
This work differs by treating adaptation as unsafe by default in a security setting, then adding a validation-selected guard around high-consequence classes.
For APT detection context, TSE-APT provides a recent ensemble-learning reference point \cite{cheng2025tseapt}, while provenance-based systems such as MAGIC and Kairos motivate downstream graph and host-provenance directions \cite{jia2024magic,cheng2024kairos}.

\section{Method}

The frozen detector is a support-floor MLP trained on the trusted Unraveled pipeline.
The selected adaptation method is batch-normalization adaptation.
During inference, the method processes the stream in a single pass with batch size 4096, dropout disabled, batch-normalization layers in train mode, default batch-normalization momentum, and checkpoint reloads per split and method.

The gate is named \texttt{recon\_guarded}.
It preserves confident frozen Data Exfiltration predictions and permits overrides only for uncertain frozen predictions when adapted confidence is sufficient.
This policy was selected on validation artifacts before final test replay.

\section{Robustness and Limitations}

The seven-seed defense addendum preserves the core Reconnaissance recovery but exposes meaningful Data Exfiltration variance.
For that reason, the original locked replay remains primary and the seven-seed result is treated as robustness evidence.
Validation-distribution sensitivity also matters: test-like validation subsamples mostly preserve the effect, but threshold choice is not perfectly stable.
External validity is limited by DAPT2020, where the detector recipe transfers but TTA is negative.

\section{Conclusion}

Selective no-label TTA can recover a rare APT stage under a locked held-out source-file split when adaptation is constrained by a validation-selected safety gate.
The strongest version of the claim is narrow and practical: the method improves the final decision policy for Reconnaissance without relying on labels at test time and without being reducible to confidence rejection.
The result should be submitted as a constrained security-ML adaptation finding, with DAPT2020 retained as an honest boundary on generality.
