Objective: identify an observable distinction between AI and human cyber-task
activity that might help SOC analysts. A behavior need not be unique to AI.
Ordinary adaptive behavior remains a candidate if its rate or pattern differs
reliably. Do not discard it merely because both groups exhibit it, and do not
accept it merely because it reflects real adaptation.

qualification audit at commit 1621cc5. Four transitions per session. No selection
based on the new aligned-versus-rotated result. No classifier fits or thresholds.
Use the unchanged exact-literal rule and association/normalization code.
Compute all five cyclic alignments, including true alignment; rotations use
only the five already observed outputs and recompute prior-output novelty.

Primary measurement: fraction of four transitions with a hit. Also report
opportunity fraction, used-literal counts, hit/opportunity when defined, and
true-minus-mean-rotated hit fraction. Preserve undefined denominators.
Compare H/A within each benchmark/task; give equal weight to tasks, average
within origin/task if multiple sessions, report all per-task signs and ranges.
This is a descriptive paired contrast, not an independent efficacy test. Do not
optimize the direction of a rule after observing the contrast. A different
direction is a new hypothesis, not a validated classifier.

Interpretation: true alignment exceeding rotations supports sensitivity to
sequence relationships; it does not itself distinguish origin. An H/A contrast
suggests a candidate to validate, not maliciousness, APT identity or analyst benefit.
A small difference can count if it is reproducible and survives task, operator,
format, opportunity and automation controls. Six tasks cannot establish that.

Stop rule: finish this measurement and diagnostic before considering a fitted
