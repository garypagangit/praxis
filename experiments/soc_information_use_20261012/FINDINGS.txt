INFORMATION USE AND HUMAN/AI DISTINCTION -- 2026-10-12

Decision
Keep adaptive behavior as a candidate Praxis direction. This exact-literal feature
does not yet demonstrate a reliable human/AI distinction. The diagnostic uncovered
measurement and observation problems that must be fixed before interpreting a
small origin difference. No classifier was fitted and no analyst benefit is claimed.
A behavior shared by humans and AI can still distinguish them probabilistically;
uniqueness is not a requirement. We are testing useful differences, not stereotypes.

Experiment completed
Rehydrated 1,152 previously hashed submission/result pairs from the broader matched
cohort, verifying 73 raw source files. Evaluated the fixed twelve-session cohort:
six paired tasks, five submissions per session, four transitions, every nonidentity
cyclic rotation of the five observed results. No later output was introduced.
Selected 26 internal-review cases across 20 populated origin/benchmark/syntax/hit
strata, including every original primary positive. Inspected excerpts and literal
witnesses, and checked suspicious pasted human input directly against raw recording.
This was an internal coding-assistant review, not independent semantic annotation.

Initial diagnostic (historical; qualification defect found afterward)
                          Human                  AI
Aligned hits              5/24 = 20.83%           4/24 = 16.67%
Mean rotated hit rate      2.08%                  7.29%
Aligned minus rotated     18.75 percentage points 9.38 percentage points

The pooled alignment difference suggested sequence sensitivity in both groups.
It did not show that the behavior identifies AI. AI-minus-human aligned rate was
-4.17 percentage points: two tasks favored H, one favored A and three tied.
Alignment excess favored H in five of six tasks, but that is not a validated
detector: source observations and feature validity fail stronger scrutiny below.
Rotations are dependent negative controls, not additional independent sessions.

Qualification correction
Review identified an AI tool truncation wrapper missed by the previous filter.
Scanning every primary output found it in FOUR of the six AI sessions, not only
the first reviewed example. Excluding affected sessions and their unmatched human
counterparts leaves TWO matched tasks (two H, two A), all CVEBench. No replacement
tasks were selected. The old six-pair coverage is superseded for this diagnostic.
On the corrected subset: H 1/8 aligned transitions, A 0/8; mean rotated rates
3.125% each. Four sessions cannot substantiate origin discrimination or transfer.
This is a comparability/completeness problem, not evidence that AI cannot adapt.
Our exclusion policy requires complete results; truncated text can still contain
valid local witnesses, but its missing history prevents a complete-novelty claim.
A future partial-observation protocol could retain such evidence with explicit
unknowns. Do not call this conservative exclusion a universal ban on truncated logs.

Measurement findings relevant to the objective
1. Real returned filename/path use occurs in both sources. It remains potentially
   useful if a stable conditional pattern distinguishes them on comparable tasks.
2. A human copied a directory listing as a submission; raw input confirms the paste
   and the next output reports command-not-found. Exact reuse is not necessarily
   helpful adaptation, although mistakes might themselves be useful future signals.
3. Three original human positives reuse the same setup filename. On the corrected
   two-task cohort, excluding the prespecified-in-correction setup-name set removes
   the sole H hit, giving 0/8 aligned hits in both groups. This post-review sensitivity
   is not an optimized filter or a result to promote as a new successful detector.
4. The narrow rule misses meaningful behavior: a human transforms returned numeric
   constants into a script; an AI switches tools after a missing executable. It
   also treats HTML closing tags and dotted code identifiers as candidate entities.
5. Exact spelling misses equivalence such as a filename versus ./filename and
   names embedded in URLs. Commands bundled into scripts can consume returned
   values internally, invisible to a next-submission literal-use measure. These
   omissions may affect H/A differently. A zero is not proof of no adaptation.
6. H/A task names overlap, but local setup exploration versus remote interaction,
   output limits, installed utilities and compound-command sizes are not controlled.
   Classification could learn those source differences rather than AI involvement.

What can currently be supported
The frozen lexical measure can be reproduced, and some hits have direct visible
text witnesses. Its coverage and meaning are insufficient to stand for adaptation
or to establish an origin discriminator in this small comparison. The original
qualification overstated complete-output coverage; that correction is explicit.
Neither shared behavior nor a weak small-sample result invalidates the broader idea.

Praxis candidate to retain
OUTPUT-CONDITIONED ADAPTATION PATTERNS FOR EVIDENCE-AWARE SOC TRIAGE.
Question: after comparable observable feedback, do humans and runtime AI differ
in how they select, transform, verify or correct actions, beyond task and tool
opportunity? A modest incremental improvement over commands and prior error
features would count. Feedback analysis itself is prior art; the application,
controlled evaluation and demonstrated analyst-useful evidence would be the
contribution. No claim of new global novelty is made by this diagnostic.

Next useful experiment
Use the measurement codebook in NEXT_COMPARISON.txt to validate feedback/action
categories against source examples, with explicit uncertainty. Audit published
records for equivalent actionable opportunities and partial-output bounds. Then
freeze a small human/AI comparison on that qualified subset. If public data cannot
provide it, collect only the missing matched episodes, not an indiscriminate large
dataset. Do not fit another model to the four remaining complete sessions.
No human participants or independent reviewers are arranged. Scripted automation
is needed to test specificity for realistic SOC use; it does not preclude an
initial bounded human-versus-AI study. Analyst assistance remains a later test.
