# Evidence sources and novelty screen

Reviewed 7 October 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive priority search. No author was contacted and no embargo or access restriction was bypassed.

| Primary source | Overlap with our candidates | Remaining distinction to establish |
|---|---|---|
| [TRACE, Ediga and Chattopadhyay](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01186v1) | Command-family fingerprints, prefixes, argument removal, scaffold transfer and active forensics already exist. | A qualified observation contract with reliable failure behavior would need to improve on these evaluations. The linked anonymous artifact returned HTTP403 from this environment; original TRACE data was not obtained. |
| [Honey for the Agent, DTU publication record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/honey-for-the-agent-cyber-deception-and-behavioral-fingerprinting/) and [dataset v1](https://zenodo.org/records/20818246) | Behavioral fingerprints across nine models, six prompts and five environments are the source study's subject. | The institutional full text is embargoed until18September2027. Exact analysis overlap remains unresolved. Our reanalysis cannot support an unqualified novelty claim. |
| [LIDAR: Who Is Behind the Harness?](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28559v1) | Agentic failure recovery and verification are already fingerprint signals in active probing. | Passive output-conditioned recovery in cyber logs must show incremental value beyond command shape, error frequency and harness cues. |
| [Known By Their Actions](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14786v1) | Passive browser behavior, timing sensitivity, early decisions and unfamiliar-agent identification overlap with generic feature/rejection ideas. | Domain transfer and a specific validated defensive decision rule are required; adding an unknown class alone is not sufficient. |
| [OHAI, CMU SEI](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/observational-humanai-ohai-a-defender-attribution-framework-for-distinguishing-human-vs-ai-threats/) | Probabilistic human-to-autonomous attribution and observable behavioral indicators already motivate this work. | Our measurement audit and held-out tests must establish detector performance, rather than assume a heuristic is an identifier. |
| [How Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocols Shape Conclusions in Provenance-Based Intrusion Detection](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01454) | Calibration, semantic completeness, alerting versus investigation metrics, and simple-baseline comparisons already have direct prior coverage. | A missing-telemetry/alert-budget contribution needs an effective mitigation and independent chronological validation, not just another metric comparison. |
| [MAGIC official implementation](https://github.com/FDUDSDE/MAGIC) | Learned provenance detection and prepared DARPA E3 graphs are existing methods/data. | Our static edge-loss screen and reused local GIN scores do not reproduce MAGIC or establish attack-chain detection. |

## Input provenance and rights

Honey input is the same archive and deterministic cache as [the preceding study](../operator_signals_20261007/README.md). Its archive SHA256 is `2b5410d5fa6d9aed5218bcaa925686af639b50f4922c13131f746d14798daeb4`. Raw Honey transcript text is not republished. Served family identity is used; this is not a claim about distillation ancestry.

Lyptus source: Jack Payne, Jeremy Miller and Sean Peters, [Offensive Cybersecurity Time Horizons](https://lyptusresearch.org/research/offensive-cyber-time-horizons), [data repository](https://github.com/lyptus-research/cyber-task-horizons-data), revision `178c0da1800f9c07311e1b8d6c61dd8104e7b4ae`. Data are CC-BY4.0, code MIT. The study evaluates bounded offensive tasks; it is not a complete real-world APT campaign dataset. Published replay input files are transformed public-data derivatives, with source hashes and original attribution retained. We use the prior PX121 AI extraction and PX122 human reconstruction from local code commit `1652f92c94c833979039b68213971a307eb6f8d1`. Reconstruction is developer-audited, not independently certified. Its longer-task transfer results are historical context, not new results of this batch.

CADETS/THEIA inputs come from MAGIC revision `aa0b647eea74b6faa0e52eb444370c4411a32cbe`. [MIT license](https://github.com/FDUDSDE/MAGIC/blob/aa0b647eea74b6faa0e52eb444370c4411a32cbe/LICENSE). `GRAPH_SOURCE_MANIFEST.json` retains decoder/source-array hashes, label assumptions and missing-field limitations. Scores from the September20 local embedding study are checked against that study's saved artifact hashes before new budget calculations. Prepared tensors omit chronology and original entity UUIDs. Unannotated nodes are benchmark negatives, not independently certified benign activity.

[OpTC's original release](https://github.com/FiveDirections/OpTC-data) provides another possible chronological source, but its full raw corpus was not acquired in this screen. We do not substitute these prepared E3 graphs for a completed OpTC chain evaluation.

The local September20 native-graph work had already investigated random edge loss. That overlap with our own prior work further narrows novelty. The new relation-removal and fixed-budget/tie analyses are extensions, not a wholly new research direction.
