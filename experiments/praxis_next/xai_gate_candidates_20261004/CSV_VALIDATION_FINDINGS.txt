PX-108: timestamped CSV validation and observational-pilot decision
4 October 2026

DECISION
Do not train the proposed exfiltration-recovery baseline from these CSVs yet.
The new files are readable and timestamp-linked, but the attack-directory rows
are overwhelmingly not standard port-53 DNS traffic. File membership cannot
stand in for verified exfiltration-row labels. This result is independent of
the earlier missing receiver-hash requirement: even an author-labeled activity
study still needs defensible mapping from rows to the target attack behavior.
Headroom remains NOT EVALUABLE, not zero. No model fitted; no AWS job launched.

ACTUAL RESULTS
All 18 stateless CSVs: 757,211 rows.
Exact timestamp match to a unique packet: 672,491 rows.
Timestamp matches more than one packet: 84,720 rows.
Unmatched timestamps: 0.
No timestamp offsets were fitted and no row-position joins were used.
The CSVs mix whole-second and fractional-second timestamp representations;
the parser accepts both without modifying the values. All timestamps parse.
No row-level label/class/attack/malicious column is present in these CSVs.

Attack-directory CSVs alone: 294,353 rows across 12 files.
Of these, 244,783 have unique timestamp matches and 49,570 are ambiguous.
Among uniquely matched rows:
  243,927 = UDP with neither endpoint on port 53 (99.65%).
      835 = UDP destination port 53.
       21 = UDP source port 53.
Observed non-53 traffic includes port pairs involving 5355, 5353 and 137.
These are measurements of packet headers, not declarations that every non-53
packet is benign. Port 53 alone also does not establish exfiltration.
The ambiguous rows are not assigned to whichever packet supports a desired label.

The 835 uniquely matched attack-file query rows equal the previously measured
835 complete question sections. The CSVs therefore do not establish coverage
of the 251,612 incomplete/invalid port-53 question sections in attack captures.
Do not infer that every missing row is malicious, or that the paper's results
are invalid; this audit establishes a coverage/label problem for OUR intended
DNS-exfiltration recovery evaluation on the downloaded release.

FEATURE CHECK
For all 173,412 uniquely matched rows with a decodable port-53 question across
attack and benign captures, publisher FQDN_count equals decoded query-name
length. This is useful evidence that this feature is aligned in the observable
subset. It does not validate entropy, all other features, unseen truncated
questions, successful transfer, or attack membership. No nonfinite numeric
cells were found in the audited numeric columns. Named identifier columns
(sld, longest_word) were not treated as numeric and must not leak capture identity.

INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION
align_stateless.py uses dpkt for packet decoding.
audit_alignment_ports.py independently reads raw Ethernet/IPv4/IPv6/UDP header
bytes and repeats the unique-time port classification.
All 54 per-file protocol-count comparisons agree (18 files x 3 categories).
STATELESS_ALIGNMENT.json and ALIGNMENT_PORT_AUDIT.json contain the evidence.
No downloaded code executed; no stateful CSVs joined; no predictions generated.

WHAT THIS CHANGES
The provisional hope that publisher CSVs would restore the missing attack-DNS
features is not supported for the required rows by this audit. They are still
usable for source/feature studies and potentially a carefully scoped capture-
classification task, but that would not establish recovery of missed exfiltration.
Training from directory labels could reward recognition of collection context.
We have not measured that possible shortcut's predictive performance.

NEXT VALID PATH
Keep this CIC release as a documented external-source qualification result.
For an exfiltration-recovery experiment, obtain corrected attack-row mapping and
complete features from the source, or build an isolated controlled DNS transfer
dataset with benign workloads, complete captures, execution IDs and verified
receipt. Any self-generated dataset must be identified as such; it demonstrates
a controlled mechanism, not independent real-world APT generalization.
The previously frozen N=3, B=5% headroom requirement remains unchanged for the
verified-transfer study. No weaker detector or substitute labels were chosen
after discovering the coverage problem. PX-109 still waits on its valid-transfer arm.
