Both captures use a 96-byte snap length. An incomplete packet has fewer stored
bytes than its recorded original length. This is a source collection limitation,
not evidence of a damaged ZIP. It restricts claims about full payload recovery.
The two capture intervals total about 14.93 hours; this is not a measured number
of independent benign host-hours. Their 530 backward timestamp steps require a
documented ordering rule before past-only history is computed.

The independent decoder found 84,040 UDP destination-port-53 packets, including
84,000 decodable DNS queries and 40 DNS parsing errors. These are bounded decoder
results, not an assertion that every possible DNS packet was identified.

Original stateless CSVs contain timestamps and total 221,073 rows. Stateful CSVs
contain 86,998 rows and no timestamp column. They cannot simply be joined by row
number, and packet-to-feature/window alignment has not been established.

RECOMMENDATION
Continue source qualification, then run a small PX-108 feasibility comparison.
Proceed with PX-109 only after transfer validity can be demonstrated. Use a
shared $50 first-stage ceiling within the already authorized $1,000 total cap.
If the publication-quality claim requires verified payload-preserving transfers,
truncated captures alone may be insufficient; use an isolated dummy-file sender/
receiver experiment with recorded hashes. Existing CIC data can still support a
limited observational comparison, subject to label and split qualification.

The key success criterion is more missed executions surfaced at equal workload
than the strongest simple method. A convincing SHAP plot, a stable explanation,
or passing an arithmetic check is not that result.
