PX-108 / PX-109: Can explanations help surface missed exfiltration?
4 October 2026 | Registered feasibility proposals; not completed efficacy studies

DECISION IN SIMPLE WORDS
Both ideas are worth a small feasibility test, but their broad novelty claims are
already contradicted by published work. Our possible contribution is narrower:
use explanation evidence to select otherwise missed data-transfer episodes for
review, and show that this works better than ordinary prediction scores at the
same review workload. Applying SHAP to another dataset is not enough.

Priority for implementation: PX-108 first because it reuses ordinary captures
and requires fewer moving parts. PX-109 has a clearer traffic-validity question
but needs verified transfers and a controlled traffic-generation harness.
Neither is yet a confirmed novel or defense-ready Praxis.

COMMON PROBLEM STATEMENT
A network detector can label a data transfer benign even when it carries stolen
data. A SOC then receives no warning. An explanation may expose an unsupported
benign decision, but explanation quality alone does not establish that a transfer
is malicious. We need to measure whether acting on explanation information puts
more real exfiltration episodes into a limited review queue than simpler scores.

PIPELINE
Observed traffic -> frozen LightGBM detector -> existing warnings retained
                                           -> otherwise silent cases
                                              -> explanation or baseline score
                                              -> equal-sized review queue
                                              -> measure labeled episodes surfaced

An entry in the review queue is an opportunity to investigate. It is not a
confirmed analyst detection. No human performance or financial savings will be
claimed from label-based replay. A failed explanation check never deletes an
existing warning. All methods use identical traffic and grouping rules.

PX-108: Testing Evidence-Validity Gates for Exfiltration Triage
Question: Can checking whether the model responds to the evidence highlighted by
its explanation help select missed transfers for review?

Technique: Explain the fixed exfiltration-versus-benign model margin using
TreeSHAP. Group dependent features into DNS syntax, volume/direction, timing,
and peer behavior. Apply supported group replacements, recomputing dependent
features together. Compare the margin response from replacing the explanation's
top group with responses from matched random groups. A low agreement score or
unsupported replacement may trigger referral; unsupported cases must count
against coverage and workload, not disappear from evaluation.

This tests model-response agreement, not the true cause of an attack. SHAP
additivity is a useful implementation check but cannot establish this claim.
Attack labels do not provide the correct feature-attribution vector. Okhankhuele's
real/synthetic ranking parity is a different, aggregate agreement test.

RQ1 / H1: Does the explanation-based selector surface more initially missed
exfiltration executions than the strongest calibrated non-XAI selector at an
equal review-case budget? H1 predicts a positive paired difference on independent
confirmation executions. Report a 95% execution-clustered interval and raw counts.
RQ2 / H2: Does explanation content cause the measured selection advantage?
H2 predicts lower recovery after removing or shuffling attributions, with all
other inputs, capacity, and review budget held fixed.
RQ3 / H3: Does the advantage persist with coherent group replacements and a held-
out execution/configuration? H3 predicts positive recovery gain in that arm.
If it exists only with arbitrary column deletion, reject the proposed claim.

Baselines: confidence; random; generic anomaly; equal-capacity full-feature
selector; conventional deletion fidelity; proposed score; proposed score with
removed/shuffled attributions. Select the strongest baseline on calibration,
never by choosing the one easiest to beat on confirmation data.

PX-109: Testing Explanation-Based Review Gates Under Valid Exfiltration Changes
Question: When a successful transfer changes its timing or permitted batching,
can explanation instability expose missed transfers better than ordinary model
scores or their instability?

Technique: First test documented original captures. Then use harmless dummy-file
transfers in an isolated sender/receiver harness. Change pacing/batching within
declared bounds; verify successful reconstruction using sender/receiver hashes.
Re-extract all features from recorded traffic. An edited PCAP is an offline
sensitivity test until successful execution has been independently verified.

The runtime score uses only the incoming observation and a frozen set of allowed
local probes. It measures attribution dispersion and top-group disagreement.
It must not require the unavailable original clean attack. Near-zero attribution
mass and unsupported probes are explicit abstention states. Instability can be
legitimate; it is a review signal, not automatic proof of explanation failure.

RQ1 / H1: At the same review budget, does the explanation-enhanced selector surface
more missed verified transfers than a score-only selector? H1 predicts a positive
paired recovery difference on independent confirmation executions.
RQ2 / H2: Is that benefit specific to explanations? H2 predicts improvement over
prediction sensitivity under identical probes, and reduced benefit with shuffled
attributions. Include an equal-capacity monitor of the original features.
RQ3 / H3: Does the benefit survive held-out shaping settings and a bounded test
against the full detector-plus-gate? H3 predicts a positive paired difference in
that arm. Search and probe budgets are frozen before confirmation.

Controls: ordinary network variation; original transfers; valid modified
transfers; invalid/incomplete transfers separately counted. Report both changed-
prediction and unchanged-prediction cases. A clean/adversarial paired SHAP drift
method may be an offline oracle comparator but is not a deployable runtime gate.

COMMON DATA AND SPLIT REQUIREMENTS
Primary candidate: CIC-Bell-DNS-EXF-2021, official raw captures and original CSVs.
It represents laboratory DNS exfiltration, one APT behavior, not full APT campaigns.
Check execution identifiers, direction, timing, tool/configuration, benign
coverage, truncation, duplicate packets and collection shortcuts before fitting.
Do not count capture files, random seeds, or transformed copies as independent
attack executions. Keep all derivatives of one execution in one split.

Reserve entire executions for confirmation before model development. No random
row split. Exclude literal domain names, endpoint addresses, date and file names
from model inputs; evaluate remaining collection artifacts. Reconstruct past-only
history from timestamps. No future packets or completed-transfer features in a
claim about earlier warnings. Published stateful CSVs need their window and row
alignment established before combining them with packet-level CSVs.

No recovery headroom means stop: the development baseline must actually miss
independent episodes under a prespecified operating-point rule. Never inspect
confirmation outcomes and then weaken the detector to manufacture misses.
AIT's existing fully warned episode proxies cannot establish a recovery benefit.

OPERATING POINT AND METRICS
Use three fitting seeds as sensitivity checks, not independent samples.
Primary pilot review budget: 5% of initially silent cases; 1% and 10% secondary.
This is a research budget, not a measured SOC capacity. Establish the case-window
size, tie rules and threshold on calibration only. Freeze exact definitions,
split manifests, feature groups, software, seeds and hashes before confirmation.
Report realized queue size and overflow when a calibration threshold is deployed;
also compare retrospective equal-budget rankings with their status made explicit.

Primary: distinct initially missed executions surfaced at the same case budget.
Secondary: total execution warning coverage, benign referrals, false-alert rate,
time to first warning, bytes before warning when verifiable, latency, probe cost,
abstention coverage, valid-transfer rate, and preservation of existing warnings.
Use paired execution-level uncertainty estimates; report insufficient power when
execution counts are small. More packets do not solve too few independent runs.
Keep diagnostic attribution-fidelity scores separate from operational recovery.

COMPUTE AND STOP RULES
One shared initial ceiling of $50 across PX-108 and PX-109, within the user's
existing $1,000 total cap. Local qualification first. CPU LightGBM/TreeSHAP;
no LLM/GPU required for these pilots. No new paid jobs launched for these proposals.
Fit the small baseline only after source qualification. Do not fit XAI selectors
until development headroom checks pass. Confirmation data remain untouched.
Stop expansion if XAI ties confidence/full-feature selection, the explanation
ablation makes no difference, valid perturbations erase the result, or independent
executions are unavailable. Retain negative findings in the registry.

WHAT THE LITERATURE CHECK ESTABLISHED
1. SCVIC-APT work uses SHAP feature selection as well as plots. The supplied
   characterization was too narrow.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07480
2. Maseno et al. already audit fidelity, stability and spurious features in IDS.
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s11416-026-00664-7
3. HiFi-XAI addresses explanation fidelity and dependent features.
   https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/195167
4. Confusion-Aware Explainable Alert Triage is a September 2026 preprint combining
   explanation testing and reliability routing; it reports a confidence-baseline
   tie in error coverage. It is not yet peer reviewed.
   https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.0988
5. Maseno et al. already test explanation drift, top-k overlap, a rule-based gate
   and a learned detector under adversarial IDS perturbations.
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s10207-026-01299-x
6. Rajhans and Khawarey already test TreeSHAP instability, including unchanged
   predictions. Their July 2026 preprint leaves network-validity constraints open.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01679v1
7. Dataset foundation and collection protocol:
   https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/dns-exf-2021.html

The possible research contribution is a tested incremental episode-recovery
benefit under valid traffic, independent executions and matched workload. This
is a hypothesis to investigate, not a verified novelty claim.
