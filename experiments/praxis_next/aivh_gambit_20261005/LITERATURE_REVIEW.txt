Autonomous AI versus human-operated attacks: literature and feature review
Reviewed 5 October 2026

DECISION
Worth a bounded next experiment, but not yet a defense-ready claim that we can
identify autonomous APTs. Passive command fingerprinting already exists. The
candidate contribution is reliable human-versus-autonomous-AI classification
across unseen operators, model families and environments, with an explicit
human false-alert limit and explanations whose dependence on source artifacts
is tested. Search did not establish priority for that complete contribution.

CLOSEST PRECEDENTS
1. Ediga and Chattopadhyay, Trace (May 2026, arXiv preprint).
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01186v1
Passive terminal TF-IDF/LinearSVC identifies seven AI model families before
active prompt injection. Reported macro-F1 .981, unseen-scaffold .815. Its target
is model family; its evaluation does not establish human-versus-AI accuracy.
It already includes command idioms, early prefixes and verb-only ablation.
Neither passive fingerprinting nor using command sequences is our novelty.
Section 3 says full data will be released later; scripts are linked.

2. Maddaloni, Safargalieva and Vasilomanolakis, Honey for the Agent.
https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/honey-for-the-agent-cyber-deception-and-behavioral-fingerprinting/
Institutional record: peer-reviewed AD&D 2026 proceedings, accepted/in press.
Abstract verified; full text embargoed until September 2027, not reviewed.
Reports model fingerprints across five environments, nine models and six
prompts; environmental cues change command selection. Therefore command
frequency is a supported candidate, but also an environment-sensitive one.
Public dataset record is dated June 23, 2026, not August:
https://zenodo.org/records/20818246

3. Known By Their Actions (May 2026, arXiv preprint).
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14786v1
Passive browser events identify underlying AI models; it assumes an automated
client. Adjacent precedent for behavioral attribution, not a cyberattack
human-versus-AI validation. Timing and structural features, with released
agent traces. It prevents claiming behavioral AI identification broadly new.

USER'S OTHER SOURCES: ROLE AND LIMITS
LLM Agent Honeypot (2024, revised February 2025):
https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13919v2
Active prompt injection plus timing. Reports 8 potential detections in
8,130,731 attempts, and illustrates one fast detection meeting both checks.
Its operational 'confirmed' category is not independently known operator
identity. This is evidence of a detection approach, not our passive benchmark.

Beelzebub, Catching AI Red Teamers in the Wild:
https://beelzebub.ai/blog/catching-ai-red-teamers-in-the-wild/
Vendor case report, not a controlled classifier evaluation. Tool switching
and reaction to hidden instructions are observed clues; neither alone proves
autonomous AI operation against a known human comparison group.

Patent 12,688,284:
https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week29/OG/patentee/alphaW.html
Existence verified in USPTO index (Mirsky, Ayzenshteyn, Weiss). Supplied PDF
and Google Patents fetch failed. Specific claim scope remains unverified;
do not treat the supplied summary as verified or as performance evidence.

Signals in the Noise:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20610
14 expert interviews and a literature review concerning loss-of-control
monitoring. Context for observability, not a validated attack classifier.

Anthropic, Mapping AI-enabled cyber threats:
https://www.anthropic.com/research/attack-navigator
832 banned accounts, not 832 confirmed autonomous attack sessions. Useful
motivation for orchestration-aware analysis. ATT&CK labels describe behaviors;
they do not supply our required operator identity labels.

Measuring AI Agents' Progress on Multi-Step Cyber Attack Scenarios:
https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11214v2
Capability evaluation in cyber ranges. Does not establish that protocol
preference is a transferable human-versus-AI identifier.

RAND, AI agents put offensive cyber within reach of novices (June 25, 2026):
https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3892-2.html
Task-performance comparison, useful motivation and task-design precedent.
Landing page does not provide a ready labeled operator-classification corpus.

The Agent Behavior (August 2025):
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14415
Proposes behavioral models and dimensions, with case-based claims. Useful
feature hypotheses, not evidence of external validity for our classifier.

Rouxii (September 2026 preprint):
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26555v1
Tests deception-aware AI pentesters and releases data:
https://zenodo.org/records/21986588
Appendix B describes reports and full session transcripts. This disproves
'Honey is the only public AI attacker dataset.' It is a candidate independent
AI-source check, pending eligibility and command-field qualification. Fixed
operators and prompting can themselves produce fingerprints. Do not assume
all observed commands were independently chosen by an LLM. This paper does
not directly benchmark evasion of our passive classifier.

HUMAN DATA FOUNDATION
GAMBiT documents human-subject red-team activity and command histories:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12865583/
Our qualified workbook subset is a useful human source. Pairing it with Honey
still entangles operator class with collection source, task and environment.
Attack-stage labels cannot remove that confound. No suitably matched public
binary corpus was verified in this search; absence was not proved.

ACTUAL MODEL FEATURES: POST-HOC, NO NEW FITTING
FEATURE_ASSOCIATIONS.json records coefficients and held-out presence counts;
inspect_features.py reproduces it from frozen models and private records.
Ten qualifying commands per window, 1,300 AI and 61 human test windows.
For the verb model:
  pwd:       898/1300 AI windows versus 3/61 human windows
  whoami:    539/1300 versus 3/61
  uname:     595/1300 versus 0/61
  pwd ls:    699/1300 versus 2/61
  ls ls ls:  229/1300 versus 42/61 (leans human in this sample)
Lexical model's largest positive coefficient is normalized PATH, followed by
cat PATH. These can encode task and logging differences. Commands are ordinary
legitimate utilities; their presence is not proof of AI or maliciousness.
Features selected by fitted coefficient magnitude, with test frequencies
reported descriptively. They are not causal effects or independent tests.
The verb model uses unigrams as well as sequences. Its current performance
does not isolate the benefit of ordering. No timing was used.

CURRENT STATUS
Command words: recall97.62%, human-window false calls4/61, group-mean FPR8.95%.
Verb model: recall71.54%, false calls0/61; external KYPO false calls0/180.
Neither reaches both frozen main targets (80% recall,5% group-mean FPR).
Zero observed errors is not a population guarantee. Nine held-out human groups
are a small sample; independent AI-source recall remains unmeasured.

RECOMMENDED NEXT TESTS (PROPOSED, NOT RUN BY THIS REVIEW)
1. Strong baseline: adapt TRACE's specified TF-IDF/LinearSVC to our binary task.
2. Compare command frequencies, ordered sequences, and shuffled sequences.
   Freeze splits and thresholds before new evaluation. This tests whether
   order contributes beyond command choice; it is not independently novel.
3. Remove paths, readme tokens and other environment clues; report performance
   and explanation changes. Probe reliance on artifacts with fixed transforms.
4. Qualify Rouxii as external AI evidence, keeping prompts, reasoning and
   framework wrapper text out of inputs. Report eligibility losses explicitly.
5. Decisive study: obtain both classes on the same tasks and recording system,
   then hold out whole tasks/environments and operators/model families. More
   computation on existing separate sources cannot substitute for this.
6. Use exact linear contributions to explain each decision; measure additive
   reconstruction and stability under irrelevant renaming. Exact linear
   attribution is established mathematics, not a new XAI method by itself.

Keep current success targets unchanged for the existing protocol. Any revised
target belongs to a new frozen protocol and fresh evaluation. An APT-wide claim
requires multi-stage campaign evidence; ten discovery-command windows support
only the narrower observed-command attribution question.

SEARCH SCOPE
Primary linked papers, institutional records, author datasets and targeted web
searches for human/AI attacker classification, autonomous attack fingerprinting
and terminal detection. This is a focused novelty assessment, not an exhaustive
systematic review or a guarantee of publication. No paid AWS jobs launched.
