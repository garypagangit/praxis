PX-117 — Distinguishing Autonomous AI Attacks from Human-Operated Attacks
Working research design, 5 October 2026. Not a completed preregistration.

THE IDEA IN ONE SENTENCE
Give people and AI systems the same authorized intrusion tasks, record what
they do in the same way, and test whether their command sequences reveal
which sessions were operated autonomously by AI rather than by a human.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
Security teams can observe attack activity without knowing whether a human,
or an autonomous AI agent is directing it. Without a tested
way to distinguish these operating modes, teams cannot reliably use an
"autonomous AI attack" label to support investigation or response planning.

PURPOSE
Build and evaluate a simple classifier of autonomous AI versus human operation
from recorded attack behavior. Measure how often it identifies AI sessions and how often
it wrongly labels human-only activity. Analyst benefit remains a proposed use;
this study does not measure response-time savings or financial loss prevented.

CLAIM BOUNDARY
The target is mode of operation, not the identity, sponsor or intent of an APT
group. Controlled multi-stage intrusions support an APT-style emulation claim.
Identifying AI use in confirmed real-world APT campaigns requires independent
campaign labels that we do not currently have. Ordinary shell activity alone
does not establish that an APT exists or that a transfer succeeded.

TWO PRIMARY CONDITIONS, WITH A SCRIPTED CONTROL
H: Human-only. An operator performs the tasks without generative AI.
A: Autonomous agent. After an initial task assignment, an AI agent chooses
   and executes all attack steps without human guidance, command selection,
   corrective hints or intervention during the run. Predetermined infrastructure
   and logging do not count as human operation. Intervened runs are separately
   labeled and cannot silently count as fully autonomous.
S: Conventional automation without AI. Scripted runs test whether the detector
   merely recognizes automation. Report separately; do not relabel S as human.
The primary binary target is H versus A. AI-assisted human sessions are outside
the primary study. They may be a later, separately labeled boundary test if
feasible; they are not required for the current experiment.
Ground truth comes from the collection condition and run audit, never from
typing speed, a honeypot trap, or the classifier's own prediction.

SIMPLE DESIGN
1. Use an isolated, reproducible cyber range with the same task variants and
   server-side recorder for all conditions. A short multi-stage task includes
   discovery, movement between designated lab hosts, collection of dummy files
   and transfer to a designated lab receiver. Use only authorized lab resources.
2. Log submitted commands, their order, timestamps and terminal results in one
   common format. Keep operator/model IDs and AI-assistant records in a separate
   label table unavailable to the classifier. Verify dummy transfer completion
   with receiver records if making any claim about successful transfer.
3. Primary observation: the first ten submitted commands, or the entire session
   if it ends sooner. Count short and unsuccessful runs. Do not select only
   successful attacks. Compare length-only and timing-only baselines explicitly.
4. Train a simple command n-gram classifier and a small tree model using command
   structure and sequence features. Include a Trace-style TF-IDF linear model.
   Use only features observed equivalently in both classes; no model text,
   source names, file paths identifying datasets, or one-sided edit counters.
5. Choose settings and the decision threshold on development/calibration only.
   Test on reserved people, task variants and agent families. Separate all runs
   from a participant into one split; prevent near-duplicate task leakage.
   Assign human sessions by participant before constructing held-family folds.
   For environment holdouts, hold out the environment in BOTH classes.
6. Repeat with timing removed. Report uncertainty by independent participant
   and task/run blocks, not by pretending every command is an independent case.

THREE RESEARCH QUESTIONS AND PROPOSED HYPOTHESES
These operating targets are design proposals. Freeze them and an adequately
powered sample-size plan before any confirmatory outcome is examined.

RQ1: Can command behavior distinguish autonomous AI attacks from human attacks?
H1: At the fixed threshold, detect >=80% of A sessions, with <=5% false AI
    labels on H. Report both rates and their intervals.

RQ2: Does detection work on an AI model family not used for training?
H2: At the same threshold, each prespecified held-family condition retains
    >=70% detection, with <=5% false AI labels on the held-out human comparison.

RQ3: Does the detector identify behavior beyond speed?
H3: With timing removed, detection drops by no more than 10 percentage points
    for autonomous AI at the same human false-alert ceiling. Calibrate the
    timing-free model separately on development data, never on the test set.

MAIN RESULTS TABLE
Rows: timing-only; length-only; command n-grams; sequence-feature tree model.
Columns: autonomous-AI recall; human false-positive
rate; scripted-automation false-positive rate; AUROC; independent sample
counts; interval estimates. Include unseen-family and timing-free panels.
AUROC is secondary: a good average score cannot replace the required false-
alert operating point. Inconclusive intervals are not a confirmed positive.

WHAT SUCCESS WOULD MEAN
In the evaluated range and held-out conditions, the method distinguishes
autonomous AI attacks from human attacks at a stated false-alert rate.
It would not establish detection of every AI-enabled APT, hybrid human/AI
operation, or real-world campaign. The title and conclusions retain this scope.

WHAT IS OPTIONAL
No new LLM training, SHAP narrative system, deterministic wrapper or conformal
gate is needed for the primary experiment. A later abstention option may be
useful, but it must not be used to disguise poor classification or claim an
unsupported accepted-error guarantee.

DATA PLAN THAT STARTS WITH AVAILABLE RECORDS
Human development source: KYPO shell-command training logs, Zenodo 6670113.
Agent development source: Honey for the Agent, Zenodo 20818246.
These provide real recorded behaviors for source/feature qualification.
Their tasks, recording systems and populations differ. A pooled classifier
would confound operator mode with dataset source, so its accuracy cannot be
the primary result. Human records require provenance checks; unlabeled wild
honeypot activity is not assumed to be human.
Prefer reproducing a documented public training scenario for matched agent
runs if its environment/tasks can be recovered. Qualify that reconstruction
before adopting it. Otherwise collect all modes in the same small range.
New participant collection requires the institution's applicable determination;
do not invent participants or treat bot-generated traces as human data.

LITERATURE THAT SUPPORTS THE STUDY
Trace (Ediga & Chattopadhyay, 2026): passive terminal sequences already support
model-family attribution. It supplies a close baseline and prevents a claim
that passive fingerprinting itself is new.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01186

LLM Agent Honeypot (Reworr & Volkov, 2024): shows an earlier detection approach
using honeypot interventions and timing. It motivates passive comparison but
does not provide independent human labels for our new evaluation.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13919

Dataset of shell commands used by participants of hands-on cybersecurity
training (2021): a published basis for studying real human command behavior.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2021.107398
Updated public data: https://zenodo.org/records/6670113

Honey for the Agent (Maddaloni, Safargalieva & Vasilomanolakis, 2026): a recorded
agent source for development and parser qualification.
https://zenodo.org/records/20818246

Measuring AI Agents' Progress on Multi-Step Cyber Attack Scenarios (2026):
supports the multi-stage setting and documents autonomous attack capability. Its
stated human completion times are estimates, not a measured matched human
baseline; do not present them as our ground truth.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214

POTENTIAL CONTRIBUTION TO VERIFY
A matched, independently labeled test of autonomous-AI versus human attack
detection, including unfamiliar model families and ordinary scripted
automation. This is the proposed empirical contribution. A full comparison
with the nearest work is still required before claiming novelty.
