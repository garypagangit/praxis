PX-112: Deferred explanation cost and sampling assumptions
Question: When do explanations generated on request save computation or storage,
while reproducing the explanation produced at decision time?
Testable hypothesis: At the frozen request fractions, deferred explanations use
less CPU time than explaining all 200 decisions together, with exact replay.
Evidence: corrected South German Credit, 600 fit / 200 calibration / 200 test.
Measures: elapsed time, record bytes, replay difference. Fractions are scenarios.
Current result: exact replay; cheaper elapsed time only at the smallest fraction
in this one timing run. Batch computation defeats a blanket lazy-is-cheaper claim.
Praxis opportunity: limited engineering component, not a new XAI method.
Sampling bounds are established mathematics. Zero failures among 300 independent
audits gives a 95% one-sided upper bound of 0.9936%, under the binomial assumptions.
It does not establish individual correctness, future robustness or legal compliance.
No request-rate data, human-review costs, privacy test or synthetic-twin validation
was supplied. Those parts of the attachment are not claimed as tested.

PX-113: Explanation stability as a model-release gate
Question: Does rejecting changed explanations prevent harmful model updates beyond
an accuracy-only release check?
Testable hypothesis: The combined gate accepts fewer harmful updates, without
rejecting beneficial updates, than the performance-only gate on the same releases.
Evidence: 45,312 chronological Electricity records; three frozen retraining cycles.
Measures: >1 percentage-point accuracy deterioration, accepted harmful releases,
rejected beneficial releases, calibration top-three attribution overlap.
Current result: no harmful updates under the frozen definition; two beneficial
updates rejected. This batch does not support the proposed gate's benefit.
Important: this is contemporaneous market classification, not a forecast. Price
features are available in the benchmark, and its original normalization is global.
Related work already studies explanation change and retraining stability:
Meyer et al. (2023): https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/meyer23a.html
Mougan et al.: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08081
Possible later contribution: evidence of which explanation changes predict harmful
release behavior at equal rejection cost. This needs independent release examples,
stronger drift baselines and held-out confirmation; three cycles cannot supply it.

PX-114: Verification claims checked against tool receipts
Question: Can an exact receipt check reduce accepted unsupported verification claims
at the same acceptance budget as confidence ranking and a model judge?
Testable hypothesis: With the registered natural-error headroom, the receipt gate
accepts fewer unsupported verification claims at the common acceptance count.
Evidence: snapshots of ten real PyPI packages and ten deliberately nonexistent
control names; 200 local Qwen answers. No packages installed or registered.
Measures: malformed answers, unsupported claims, unique affected targets,
acceptance coverage, accepted unsupported claims, generation time.
Headroom: at least five unsupported verification claims over three targets.
If that fails, stop the efficacy claim. Injection tests cannot replace headroom.
No private reasoning traces are requested. Evidence consists of visible answers
and visible receipts. Same-model judges are correlated, not independent experts.
Very close precedent: Basu (2026), Tool Receipts, Not Zero-Knowledge Proofs:
Practical Hallucination Detection for AI Agents. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10060
This makes the broad deterministic-receipt-gate novelty claim untenable.
Name existence is not malicious-package detection, and deliberately nonexistent
controls cannot establish performance on hallucinated names that have been squatted.

PX-115: SHAP narrative consistency versus deterministic rendering
Question: What does a structured explanation check actually guarantee, and what
errors can still pass in the accompanying text?
Testable hypothesis: The checker detects wrong feature/sign/order fields but fails
to reject contradictory prose when the structured fields are correct.
Evidence: first 32 frozen test records from corrected South German Credit,
model attributions and Qwen-generated text; deterministic rendering comparator.
Measures: parseability, exact reason consistency, acceptance, injected-fault
detection and elapsed time. Natural errors and injected diagnostics stay separate.
Closest precedents:
How good is my story? https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10220
FAX: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27879
Reasoning Externalization: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38869
These already cover narrative evaluation, evidence verification and the distinction
between structured verification and unrestricted prose. German Credit plus a SHAP
checker is not first use. Corrected South German is not an independent dataset
relative to the original German Credit records.
Possible later contribution: independently measured residual contradictions at
matched coverage, with constrained rendering and published narrative-verification
baselines. A comparison with a weak ungated model alone is insufficient.
No human comprehension or human-authored cost benefit is measured in this pilot.
