An APT classifier can achieve a better overall score while warning on fewer attacks. This praxis tests that problem by reporting correct attack-stage labels, attacks called benign and false alerts together. In a UNRAVELED comparison, mean macro-F1 rose from 0.7148 to 0.7379 while exfiltration warning recall fell from 85.18% to 76.25%. The size of the difference depended strongly on the fitting seed. An adapted AIT test on two executions excluded from training did not reproduce the original policy ordering.

Follow-up studies examined possible repairs. A deterministic OR rule preserves a warning whenever any member model warns. Adding more models recovered some flows but no additional time-defined exfiltration episodes in the tested sources. On UNRAVELED, twelve wholly missed episode proxies were twelve singleton flows from one endpoint pair. Adding a TCP/22 policy warning raised episode coverage from 6/18 to 18/18, with 510 additional benign-labeled flow warnings and 460 additional grouped cases. The same rule added no exfiltration coverage on AIT, where the labeled exfiltration used UDP/53. These repairs used previously examined data.

The contribution is a reproducible model-review procedure: verify labels and chronology, compare the same records, distinguish wrong-stage warnings from attacks called benign, and report workload alongside coverage. Laboratory labels, related flows and simulated handling times limit claims about independent incidents, early detection and operational benefit.
