## Selected contribution

**When Better APT Scores Hide Missed Attack Warnings** is an applied evaluation study.

**Primary question:** When do better APT model scores come with more missed attack warnings?

**Contribution:** Controlled measurements of the relationship between overall classification scores, correct stage labels, attacks classified as benign, and benign false alerts, together with an executable evaluation and source-qualification procedure.

The warning-loss finding is the central story. The temporal experiment and dataset-qualification audit support the credibility and applicability of that story. The history-selector and evidence-acquisition models are experimental comparisons used to investigate the question.

Existing literature already describes temporal evaluation problems and attack-to-normal errors. The contribution rests on the specific controlled evidence and usable audit artifacts, not a claim to originate those concepts. The [literature positioning audit](measurement_praxis/LITERATURE_AND_CLAIMS.md) records the closest work and the distinctions.

## How to evaluate progress

- Explain one practical decision problem in plain language.
- State what the completed observations add to the closest literature.
- Connect every empirical claim to a specified comparison, denominator and result artifact.
- Retain unfavorable outcomes, seed variation and source limitations.
- Distinguish measured classification behavior from unmeasured analyst or deployment benefits.
- Assess manuscript clarity and evidence sufficiency for the intended review.

Algorithmic superiority must not become an additional success gate. New architectures or datasets should be introduced only to answer a concrete scientific question or resolve a material evidence limitation. An unsuccessful model comparison can still supply informative evidence for the evaluation question; it must not be relabeled as detector improvement.
