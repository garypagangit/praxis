PILOT DECISION RULE -- ENGINEERING/RESEARCH SCREEN, NOT A SIGNIFICANCE TEST

Keep the objective: a useful human/AI distinction for SOC evidence assessment.
Do not require behavior to be unique to AI, a large improvement, or a transformer.

Gate 1: collection can support the comparison
At least 95% of accepted submissions have complete command/result/status records;
all truncation is explicit; no provenance in defender inputs; verified origin for
all efficacy cases; identical task/variant/recorder allocation across H/A.
Primary five-command coverage and lost sessions must be reported by origin.
If a systematic asymmetry exists, repair it before looking for an origin signal.
These are engineering targets chosen for this pilot, not externally mandated rules.

Gate 2: the behavior can be scored
At least 80% raw independent reviewer agreement on each candidate category among
decidable double-coded episodes, with opportunity/unknown coverage reported. This
is a pilot screen, not a semantic-accuracy estimate. Inspect disagreement even when
agreement exceeds 80%. If nearly all responses are identical, report a ceiling/floor
problem and redesign tasks before model training. Do not treat that as proof that
real attack behavior lacks a difference.

Gate 3: a candidate is worth confirmation
Report all four prespecified categories. A category is promising for follow-up if
the H/A contrast has the same nonzero direction in at least three of four task
families with observable opportunities, is not attributable to one human or one AI
configuration, and persists after descriptive opportunity/action-count controls.
No minimum large effect is imposed. This 3-of-4 rule is a heuristic screening rule,
not statistical proof; selection among categories requires fresh confirmation.
If too few task families have the relevant opportunity, the category is unresolved,
not passed or rejected. Extend targeted coverage rather than mine unrelated features.
Report ordinary script behavior separately; similarity to AI does not refute H/A
differences, but blocks claims of AI specificity against ordinary automation.

Decision
PROMISING: plan a powered/precision-based independent comparison and realistic
attack-environment transfer. Freeze one primary contrast, operating point and
smallest useful gain before that test. No winning Praxis claim yet.
UNRESOLVED: repair a named measurement/coverage problem once, retaining both versions.
NO PROMISING SIGNAL: stop promoting these features after a fair evaluable pilot;
review another candidate in the portfolio instead of endlessly expanding features.

What constitutes a defensible positive Praxis
An increment over a credible baseline on new independent units, with quantified
uncertainty and false-positive/coverage tradeoffs, surviving relevant controls and
with a justified applied contribution. To claim help to analysts, additionally
demonstrate a decision-quality or review-time benefit. A successful toy task,
high training accuracy, or engineering test passing is not that evidence.
