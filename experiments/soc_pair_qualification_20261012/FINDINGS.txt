QUALIFICATION FINDINGS -- 2026-10-12

What was checked
Reassociated raw returned text with reconstructed human shell submissions and AI
tool calls. Verified 137 source hash/size bindings: 90 human archives, three full
AI archives and 44 AI members. Individual members are verified; the 44 original
remote archives have not been fully reverified. Human command reconstruction is
inherited from the prior replay, not an independently validated ground truth.
All 2,106 in-horizon command/result comparisons agree with the prior extraction.
Among 2,327 AI event/message comparisons, two disagree (empty event result versus
missing message result); neither enters the retained first-20 blocks. Disagreement
is recorded in QUALIFICATION.json, not silently converted to empty output.

Boundary
First 20 non-control submissions, preserving entire compound commands. A result
belongs to its whole submission, not every atomic command in it. Human output is
bounded by terminal markers and the next same-stream submission. AI output needs
a unique ordered tool-call/reply association. Adjacent transitions must be from
the same human stream or completed serial AI calls; gaps are not bridged.
Only appearance-only color codes and line endings are normalized. Other terminal
controls, apparent prompts/frames, wrappers and missing text are excluded. This
qualifies a conservative plain-text subset, not all terminal output. Empty output
is distinct from missing output. Comparable exit status and timing remain absent.

The larger cohort is useful for exploratory measurement work. It is not proof
that differently recorded H/A observations are fully equivalent. The October 11
five-submission boundary remains primary; moving to three or later sessions would
be a separately labeled amendment, not a retrospective primary success.
At five submissions, supported literal candidates occur in 6/6 H and 5/6 A;
literal use occurs in 4/6 H and 3/6 A. This establishes occurrence in both groups,
not a useful AI-specific signal. No direction, significance or detector threshold
is inferred from these twelve sessions.

What is fruitful
1. Keeping compound submissions whole recovers usable collective results without
   inventing per-atomic-command observations. This offers a reproducible coverage
   and measurement study; coverage alone is not a proven research contribution.
2. Result-to-next-command literal information use is distinct from PX-125's error
   repetition/argument-change features. A small, transparent diagnostic is now
   implementable. It must survive alignment, opportunity and source controls.
3. A negative specificity result could still tell analysts which apparently
   AI-like behavior is ordinary human or scripted activity. That requires the
   appropriate controls, not relabeling failure as success.

What is not established
No operational AI detector, maliciousness classifier, APT attribution, hybrid
detection, scripted-automation specificity, external transfer, or analyst benefit.
All existing cases are exposed development data. Human skill, task allocation,
tool harness and model family can confound origin. Independent human semantic
review has not been completed; fixture tests validate code rules only.

Recommendation
Validate the literal-use measurement and its alignment control on the published
data before fitting. Seek additional compatible published human/result sessions
or reliable rendering of excluded terminal records to improve the fixed-prefix
cohort. Do not recruit a large dataset by default, replace humans with simulations,
or fit a transformer to twelve sessions. A modest credible benefit remains enough;
the current obstacle is credible comparison, not an ambitious required effect.
